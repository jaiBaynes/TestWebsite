*Myth ?.512: Pikrosa

PART 1
Ancient agricultural societies like Graia were not very sanitary. Due to crowded intermingling with animals, poor waste management and antiquated understandings of disease and medicine outbreaks of bubonic plague, influenza, typhoid fever, smallpox, epidemic typhus, and measles ran rampant. In those days a particular plague ran rampant, especially due to overcrowding caused by more and more destitute people of Olympia losing it all and being thrown outside the city walls.
Demeter was popular among the poor for her power to feed them, Apollo the god of healing was important for his role in warding off disease. As plague continued running through the slums, a great many of the poor gathered around the Temple of Apollo, praying to be healed.
They could not even afford the snake oil the temple sold. In Graia, snake oil was not just a pejorative for scam medicines: it was literally what Apollo sold as the cheapest cure-all for ailments. The snake as a symbol of healing was common so many poor souls believed its bodily oils actually had some medicinal purpose.
Those of means opted for more costly treatments. Drinking holy water from an underground spring, mud baths and hot teas of chamomile or peppermint, sacred dogs licking open wounds to ward off infections or removing any random organ a priest assumed was releasing “bad humors” to blight the body. Plenty of ritualistic options were available for those who were ailing. Though that is not to say any of these more expensive options were actually more effective.
So long as disease was believed to be the result of evil spirits and curses, no true cures could be pursued. Priests actually blessed by Apollo to lay their hands on the sick and cure them were prohibitively expensive and so none of the small temples in the slums had any.
Thus, Pikrosa, one of the few wealthy women of the slums had to make do with lesser treatments. Her legion of bodyguards cleared the sickly mob and ushered the coughing woman into the temple. There she underwent all the silly practices they had prepared for her daily routine. Her husband, zealous in his trust of the gods paid for everything the temple offered while waiting for the high-ranking priest he’d ordered to visit her. Pikrosa, her body ailing from the plague, did not match her husband’s faith.
She looked at the con artists with disgust: they were probably laughing at her, sitting there and paying them to do nothing as her cough only worsened. Pikrosa knew that everybody had an agenda and was looking to exploit their fellows; that was the rule of the slums and the whole world. That was why it was so disappointing that her husband, who was normally so crafty and wise, became so vulnerable and desperate the second she became ill. His enemies would be sure to pounce on this. As she left, she did not even thank the vile priest who continued trying to act friendly to his latest mark.
As she emerged, Pikrosa noticed that the line waiting for healing had dispersed. Many of them gathered in a crowd around a free bread stand. Strange; hadn’t the Cult of Demeter stopped the dole ages ago? Why would someone try giving out free bread now? What was their angle. The intrigued woman commanded her security detail to escort her over so she might inspect this bizarre group.
“Free bread! Come get your free bread!”  a demi human girl in strange clothing and covered in wealth and splendor announced to bring people over.
Pikrosa did not recognize the strange style of clothing she wore, but as a collector of fine jewelry she recognized the gold ornaments as being distinctly Scythian in style. The foreign, unique, abstract animal art was all the rage amongst Graiac nobility; even Pikrosa could only afford a few of those trinkets. A woman of such means mingling amongst the poor was even more suspicious. Was she with one of the other Tyrants?
A great many seats of varying quality were set up around the fresh bread stand so that the people could sit and eat comfortably while waiting for their turn in the temple. However, wasn’t it a dangerous idea to have so many sick people together? It basically guaranteed that nobody else approached for bread. Once the group ran out of bread they shifted to setting up a platform for the girl to speak on.
“Hello everybody! While you enjoy your food, please pay attention to my explanation! Today I want to talk about diseases: causes, combats and cures!” the girl began.
Pikrosa laughed, of course she was one of those. Many priests of various gods targeted the infected in slums with sermons about which moral virtues could earn the gods’ favor and ward off diseases. Donate to the temple, serve your employers or masters without complaining and most commonly pay them directly for their good deeds to receive healing. Pikrosa prepared to leave but was given pause when she heard what the girl actually had to say.
“Some say diseases are caused by invisible forces while others say that is superstitious silliness. No matter which side of the argument you’re on, both views are right and wrong in their own way.” She grinned.
“The causes of disease can’t be seen with the naked eye, but not because they’re magic or divine. Imagine an animal so small thousands of them can swim around in a drop of water!”
As the girl explained, an even younger girl held up crudely made visual aids: strange colored creatures floating in a water droplet with an arrow pointing down to a single water drop on the table.
“These little guys are all over the place and since you can’t see them, sometimes you might breathe them in through your nose or swallow them after touching something dirty. Depending on which ones enter your body they can cause different nasty effects. However, many of the sick feelings you suffer are actually from your own body trying to fight them off.” She motioned for the child to switch to the next visual aid.
Different colored creatures were now drawn with different names above them saying which specific disease they symbolized.
“They can’t handle the heat so when you are burning up with fever, that is actually your body trying to protect you by killing all the disease with temperature. Sneezing is your body trying to send them flying out of your nose so they can’t hurt you anymore.” She held up the picture depicting sneezing as the younger girl held up the picture explaining fever.
Then the girl began describing some common diseases known to spread in the slums. She gave them strange names such as bubonic plague, influenza, typhoid fever, smallpox, epidemic typhus and pneumonia. Many people recognized their symptoms and began paying attention when she listed common cures. However, what she prescribed was rarely feasible for them to attain.
She described various strange potions called “antibiotics” and “vaccines” alongside which natural ingredients they could be made using. However, the insane grocery list required was outside the means of even Pikrosa, she didn’t even know what some of the ingredients were. Thankfully for the more exotic ingredients the girl provided visual aids and descriptions of them.
Still, Pikrosa was utterly confused; what was this raving lady talking about. She didn’t believe diseases came from evil spirits or curses, but wasn’t this girl basically saying the same thing? What difference did it make if the invisible enemy was magic or just unbelievably tiny. What did it matter if curing potions were called holy tonics or antibiotics? Yet as she found herself doubting the girl, Pikrosa noticed she hadn’t coughed in a while.
“Now then, that’s all the materials I have prepared. Any questions? It’s super-duper important everybody understands all this.” She concluded.
“Why do you blaspheme against the lord Apollo lord of healing and his sacred son Asclepius!? After all they’ve done for us, you claim you can cure us instead!” a man among the sick cried.
“I didn’t say I could cure you; I said regular medicines can cure you.”
“You ascribe the powers of the divine to the mundane! Shame on you! May Apollo smite you with the greatest of all diseases in his plague arrows!”
“Yowch. Tough crowd.” She laughed.
“Do not take this lightly little girl!” the man continued yelling.
Weird, he’d normally be out of breath and wheezing by now. After all his sickness was exactly what the girl called “pneumonia.”  Actually, his fever and chest pain were gone now. And his normally low energy was replaced by fiery zeal condemning the fake healer. As realization washed over him, he silently trembled. Had he been cured somehow?
“Any other questions?” the girl smiled at him.
“What is happening? Did you do this?” he asked as many others in the crowd realized they too felt better.
“I just explained the causes and cures for some common diseases. Thankfully it seems like you understood.”
“But I- we go to lord Apollo for healing from evil spirits using sacred means! We do not believe any blasphemous lies! Apollo is our lord! We do not believe in you!”
“You don’t have to believe in me. You just have to understand. You get sick whether or not you believe in bacteria and viruses so knowledge trumps faith when it comes to diseases.” The girl smiled.
The healed people rushed off to the Temple of Apollo singing his praises and blessing the priests who they believed had finally cured them. But Pikrosa was left pondering what really happened. She noticed that everyone took Apollo’s snake oil, and everyone ate the bread so neither of those were the cure. Only those who paid attention to the girl were cured.
“Excuse me! Everyone who wasn’t healed. Can we get a show of hands for if you understood my presentation. Also, if none of the diseases I mentioned match yours please come see me and tell me what yours are.”
Many people were now interested in the girl, started paying attention and asked questions of what they had ignored or misunderstood. As she explained it again for them, they and all others with the same confusion were cured and rejoiced together. Pikrosa was stunned; as the girl said, understanding what she said about a disease seemed to cure it!? What sorcery was this?
“A great deal of you are coughing up blood. Of course. I’m sorry I should’ve realized tuberculosis would be common in the slums too. I’ll be sure to add it to my list. But for those of you who suffer the coughing of blood, it is caused by a specific bacteria called Mycobacterium tuberculosis. I don’t have any cute pictures for them but know that this bug is a lot like Mycobacterium leprae, the bacteria that causes leprosy which I talked about earlier.” She went on to explain the similarities and differences as well as the cures for tuberculosis.
When those who coughed blood understood all this, they felt immediate relief: their bodies were completely restored to health.
“Awesome! Any others who are still sick?” she asked.
Pikrosa was stunned by this spectacle, she kept her distance but was determined to investigate this mysterious arrival.

PART 2
“What are you after?” Pikrosa demanded later on.
“What do you mean?” Kat tilted her head in confusion.
She followed Kat back to the small bakery where she returned to and confronted her demanding answers.
“I’m not an idiot. You’ve done me a great service so you must be expecting something. There’s no such thing as a free lunch.”
“Sure, there is. Didn’t you take any of the free bread?”
“No, I didn’t because there is no such thing as a free lunch!”
“Fresh out of the oven, please enjoy.” Kasious placed a fresh loaf of bread on the nice new table outside his bakery.
Pikrosa looked at it skeptically. Kat dug in and offered her a slice. When she refused, Kat offered some to her various bodyguards. Following their mistress’ example they too refused.
“Seriously. Despite my apprehension, I am willing to reward you. Though considering the strange powers you wield and wealth you are adorned in, I fear I may not have anything of value to offer you.” Pikrosa stayed on topic.
“I already said I don’t want anything. You didn’t see me shake down any of the other people who were healed, did you?” Kat shrugged, “Doing the right thing can be its own reward.”
“Really? You can subsist off the satisfaction of playing a savior?”
“If you want to interpret altruism like that then sure. I don’t know why you’re so on edge; you aren’t dying anymore.” Kat laughed as she continued her snack, “Seriously, I’ll feel guilty if I eat this all myself. Anybody who wants in there, come and take!”
The baker and his various assistants emerged, taking as they pleased. There were still local shops large enough to hire employees?
“Is this shop’s success another miracle you accomplished?” Pikrosa asked.
“Not at all. It was all the hard work of these fine people. Alone they were doomed but working together their business can survive.” Kat smiled.
“With all due respect, she is addicted to downplaying her own contributions. We worked our butts off to make it this far, but you’re right to suspect she helped us get here.” Kasious insisted.
“You run this shop?” Pikrosa asked.
“Sort of. We run it together. Kat helped us work out a mutually beneficial arrangement.” He motioned towards his fellow bakers.
“You seem awfully young is all.”
“My parents used to run this place.” Kasious tensed up as painful memories came to him, but then he saw Kat’s knowing nod and continued.
“My dad died doing extra mining work to avoid selling my sister and me. And my mom… she was taken as repayment for his debts and worked to death not long after.” Kasious genuinely began tearing up.
Pikrosa froze. She had a son about his age; his plight struck her.
“But life keeps going. We’ve had a turn around and with this stroke of fortune, I think we can increase our lot in life.” Kasious wiped his tears.
“This free bread stunt might squander the big break though. Will you make enough money to keep your family business?” Pikrosa spoke with sociological callousness but psychological kindness.
“We’ll see. But after receiving incredible kindness, it only felt right to pay it forward. Don’t you agree?” Kasious smiled.
Pikrosa was given pause, reflecting on everything.
“I see.” Pikrosa smiled back, “I think I shall enjoy patronizing this bakery in the future.”
“But you didn’t even try our bread to see if you liked it…” Kasious cluelessly noted.
“Brother! Don’t turn down potential customers!” Glyka his little sister, smacked his arm.
“As a matter of fact, put some loaves in for myself and all my men.” Pikrosa couldn’t help but grin.
“Great! While we wait for the snack, let’s take a walk. Your sickness is gone but exercise is important to replenish your body.” Kat stood up.
She led Pikrosa through the slums. Pikrosa was disgusted as she saw the unsightly destitution. She thanked her stars she had escaped such depravity. That was why ever since her ascension she rarely ever returned to these wretched streets, uncomfortable by the awful memories they reminded her of.
“It’s unfortunate. They call Eupatridus the good tyrant but here in his territory there is still such suffering.” Kat sighed.
Pikrosa froze, “What?”
“This section of town is the Tyrant Eupatridus’ territory. The suffering you see around you is his doing. Though I suppose his palace’s abundant furnishings must be paid for somehow. People like Kasious don’t have their money vanish into thin air; whenever he scrounges up even a pittance it is taken by the Tyrant to fund his lavish lifestyle. Poverty does not create itself.”
Before the stunned Pikrosa could respond, they had finished their depressing walk around the block and returned to Kasious’ bakery only to find it being set upon by five thugs there to collect protection fees.

PART 3
“Right on time. This time every week Eupatridus’ goons come to shake down hard-working people like him for protection fees.” Kat shook her head.
As Kat described, the men from Eupatridus’ gang surrounded the terrified baker, with only the adorably ineffective hissing of Kat’s pet Gatola standing between them.
“Look at this; business is booming baker. This might finally cover a fair bit of your debt.” The thugs immediately grabbed at his furniture and forced their way into the bakery to inspect his savings.
“Please stop! We just got ahead! With a bit more time, we could double what we owe and still have some left. If you take all our savings now…” Kasious cried, holding his terrified sister.
“Be quiet kid! We’ve been lenient enough as is. It is time you paid your Tyrant what he is owed!” the thug growled.
“Enough!” Picrosa stepped in.
“What do you want b-” the thug began before freezing up when he saw Kat and Pikrosa.
The terrified thug was one of the duo Kat had scared off last time. He saw her “demonic strength” and knew she was utterly terrifying. That was why he brought three friends as backup when shaking Kasious down this time. And yet, the girl he swore was a demon was not what scared him most.
“Lady Pikrosa! It is an honor to be in your presence!” the thugs all knelt before her.
Kasious was utterly confused. Was this part of Kat’s plan too?
“I am ashamed of you all. Is that how servants of house Eupatridus carry themselves?” she was appalled.
The goons were utterly terrified as the matron and her band of elite bodyguards loomed over them.
“We apologize for our unsightly actions Lady Pikrosa!” they begged for forgiveness.
“Enough groveling. Just answer me truthfully… did my husband really put you up to this?” Pikrosa demanded.
