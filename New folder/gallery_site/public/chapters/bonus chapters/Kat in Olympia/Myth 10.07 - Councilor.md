*Myth ?.515: Councilor

Not long after they first met, Eupatridus began sarcastically calling Kat “councilor.”  She always had some hairbrained scheme or the other for Eupatridus to follow. Eupatridus’ harsh words softened when some of the girl’s scatterbrained suggestions started yielding results. 
“I don’t know if you’ve realized but I don’t run a charity organization.” Eupatridus scoffed when Kat suggested he order grain in bulk himself and then have bakers buy it from him at cost to save money. When purchasing in bulk he, and by extension they, saved significantly on the cost of supplies. But what exactly would Eupatridus get out of this?
“Pave the paths? Beautify storefronts? Set up signs? Girl, you remember this is a slum and not a city, right?” Eupatridus demanded after reading over some of her recommendations.
“A city reflects its ruler does it not? Why not clean it up as we clean up your image?” Kat replied. Eupatridus hand waved it away.
He marvelled as in almost no time the city visually improved. Rather than jobless people lining the streets they either had a job enabled by the money local business saved or were able to buy more from them with the money they didn’t have to pay him in protection fees. Good for them but still, what did that afford him?
“Three cheers for the good tyrant!” a man on the street cried as he recognized the tyrant. Eupatridus was shocked. His bodyguards were confused. Rather than having to keep away vengeful bums, they were surrounded by an adoring public.
“Since the streets are safer, you could probably reduce your security detail and reassign them to more valuable positions.” Kat presented a list she had drawn up that night.
“I thought I told you not to bother me outside our weekly meetings?” Eupatridus grumbled.
“Give it here dear, I’ll shuffle the staff.” Pikrosa took the list from Kat instead.
Once again, whenever she didn’t get her way, the crafty girl turned to Eupatridus’ wife. The Tyrant, still feeling guilty about lying to her was letting far too much slide. She’d stopped making him sleep in the living room, so maybe he could start to assert himself once again.
“Dad, want to attend our first community theatre show?” Theo asked awkwardly.
“First what?”
“Thanks to the subsidized tolls my friends and I can go back and forth between the big city and slums more easily.”
“Friends?” Eupatridus paused, overjoyed to hear his son say that word.
“Yeah, well whatever. Some of those nerds Kat introduced me to after the play were pretty cool so we started hanging out and it would be a big deal if we had a test audience for what we’ve been working on.”
“Of course I’ll be there!” Eupatridus smiled before pausing.
The reduction in toll prices to allow for travel between city and slums was also Kat’s suggestion. Eupatridus was more focused on the capital’s capital flowing in but hadn’t considered… was this part of her plan too?
Whether directly or by trickery that girl seemed to have him putting his name to all sorts of bizarre projects. As they proved more and more successful financially, in terms of popularity and personally for those Eupatridus loved, he listened to Kat more and more readily.
She convinced Eupatridus to construct a slide along the large outer walls which kept the slum out. It was to allow for faster travel in his territory once completed. Nobody thought it would ever be done. What a giant, wasteful project; so much expense hiring so many bums to shuffle around doing nothing. The town’s prosperity and community inexplicably increased.
Another hairbrained plan was to create a river so people wouldn’t have to walk as far for water each day. She thought one could just dig paths and divert water like that? Sure, farmers did something similar, but it was on a whole different scale than this insane idea. The celebration of the water’s completion alone was a boom in businesses. Eupatridus was the man of the hour hailed as a great reformer for his brilliance.
All the while, the councillor Kat stood by, clapping for him with the crowd. After being feared for so long, being loved in equal measure; being able to finally, proudly share his work with his wife and son who he’d kept at a distance in shame for so long… it was too good to be true.
Eupatridus was a stubborn man! He knew all this was according to that strange girl’s schemes and he would not be won over so easily by one clearly out to manipulate him for some reason.
“I have not collected protection money in over a month and your additional expenses have cost me dearly girl; what do you have to say for yourself?” Eupatridus demanded at the start of their next weekly meeting.
“But everyone seems a lot happier don’t they.” Kat smiled.
“Yes, you always seem to know exactly what the people want and how to string them along as well. Are you reading their minds or first convincing them of what they want before having me grant it?”
“Neither actually. You don’t need a telepath if you just ask people what their problems are. My suggestions and requests are the will and grievances of this slum’s population. It’s all in my report.”
“Of course. And you always seem to have the answer.”
“Not at all; you give me too much credit. I’m not that clever, but I am well read. Nothing is new under the sun; countless cities have suffered similar problems and of them even a single success story is all that is needed to avail the people here of the same problem.”
“Yes, you do love quoting from others’ example.”
“I believe history is a powerful teacher. It was not you or I who accomplished so much for the people here; it was the hardworking and ingenious problem solvers of the past.”
“You say that you’ve solved all these problems at my expense!”
“No offense, but I’ve seen what you spend your plunder on. Most of it is just making yet more money. You can’t take it all with you y’know.”
“Do you think it is charming to constantly allude to my death?”
“Apologies. I ask you forgive a quirk of my homeland’s humor.”
“I am well aware of my age girl; do you want people thinking I went mad at the end of my life”
“Isn’t it preferable for people to remember you as a wise tyrant who at the end of his life reformed and repaid all the harm he had done?”
“Your talk of legacy grows tiresome. Is a bit of popularity enough to protect my wife and son when I’m gone?”
“Nope. But it is a prerequisite for what will.”
“Enough! I will not be strung along any longer! You keep making plans, thinking I am in the palm of your hand; enough I say! I am the Tyrant Eupatridus and I will not have a little girl as my councilor! I am in charge here; you have no power over me!!!”
Kat said nothing but flashed a smug, knowing grin. At that, the proud Eupatridus nearly exploded.
“Do not take me for a fool! I know you’ve manipulated my wife and son! You think you can blackmail me with them! You twist my honor and repayment of favors to abuse my generosity. You think you can control me with the love of the masses. If I cancel your popular plans and start extorting people again their hatred would eclipse the contempt that they once held me in. You think I’ll bend to your will out of fear of open revolt! You think to maintain authority in my final days I cannot stray from the path you want to steer me down!” he roared.
“I mean…” Kat just shrugged.
“Know this, child! I am a tyrant; the butcher of Boetia. If you turn the whole world against me, my organization stands ready to cut them down! That is how I rose to power; that is how I retained power. No matter your schemes, I am more than capable of enforcing my will on these slums. I can be a hundredfold more ruthless than you hope I am kind. That is the way of tyrants; this world turns on my will. Blessings and curses are mine to bestow. I am not a womanly savior! Here I am king!”
Eupatridus was short of breath after that last rant. As he recovered his stamina he was shocked to hear his own words repeated back to him.
“This country is obsessed with contracts and agreements. Why would you consent to audio recording when we met without knowing what that means.” Kat shook her head.
“How… how did you do that?”
“The how doesn’t matter. The big question should be: what can I do with it? Your wife and son would be disappointed to hear that especially with all the connections they’ve made in town. The public too. And also, your men might not be as eager as you think to besiege the place where their families live. You’re a military man, you know getting troops to attack their own is a faux pas. You’re old, your influence is waning; your rivals would seize on the chaos to take power and be greeted as liberators from the mad tyrant.”
Kat’s points left Eupatridus stammering. This girl was finally showing her true colors. Even though the annoying friendly tone remained, she was openly stating her hostility! The Tyrant prepared a rebuttal but was interrupted by yet more of his own words.
“I am not a womanly savior! Here I am king!” Kat played back Eupatridus’ rant.
The Tyrant froze in horror; did he really say that?
“If your wife or son heard your rant, your personal life would be over. If the people did, your tyranny would be over. But… if the King of Olympia… or the King of the gods were to hear this, there would be nothing left of you. I didn’t anticipate you’d give me such a gift.” Kat warned.
Eupatridus panicked. In Graia, claiming kingship or godhood was grounds for execution. His position as a man of influence; one who might actually be a threat to the crown… made such words border on a declaration of war against the heavens themselves! It would be easy to frame him as preparing for a coup.
The gods were the unquestioned rulers of the universe, and the kings were their divinely chosen rulers. Eupatridus, in his hubris had just blasphemed and opened himself up to terrible punishment. But she was bluffing right!? There was no ways she could show King Pelops this and there was surely no way she could get the ear of the Thunderer himself!
Yet, the absolute confidence she wore shook Eupatridus to his core. Who was this girl? Though she never admitted it, she clearly had the power to heal the sick. She seemed to appear and disappear as she pleased. She had far more knowledge and skill than a child her age should have. And the tyrant’s men had reported being unable to defeat her even while armed. He’d pushed the possibility out of his mind for too long but… was it possible she was not a mere mortal?
“What do you really want?” Eupatridus growled.
“I want you to admit defeat.” Kat smiled.
A long silence passed between them.
“You’re serious?”
“Yep yep. People tell tales about you, butcher of Boeotia. But while reading your memoirs I noticed something interesting. Your praises were almost always for those who bested you. Quite the good sport, controversy be damned nobody earns your respect more than one you were unable to defeat.”
“What? Stay focused, I’m being serious!”
“And so am I. For what I have planned next, I can’t have you as a foe or a puppet. I want you as a trusted ally. Since nothing else has achieved the degree to which I desire, I must temporarily take on the role of foe and assert my dominance.”
“You’re absurd.”
“I dunno, I think I’ve got you beat.” Kat asked before replaying the damning words he said again.
Eupatridus flinched. Was she serious? Was she an enemy or not?
“You’re clever Eupatridus, but once you acknowledge that I have outsmarted you, we can proceed.” Kat appeared directly in front of him with her hand out to shake.
“Nonsense!” the tyrant was prouder than he was cunning.
He stood up to try and loom over the girl but was shocked that she disappeared. He turned around in shock to find her sitting atop his throne, not on the seat but upon the top of the back. Even as he stood, she was now higher than him.
“What are you; what strange power has come to bedevil me!” Eupatridus trembled.
“I’m just Kat; the girl who bested you.”
“Do you really think capturing my voice with your strange magic requires wit?”
“That was only part. From the day I first conceived of this scheme, I had you right where I wanted you. Think about it.”
Kat went over it from the beginning; how she’d contrived their meeting, chosen her words and actions carefully to steer everything exactly as she wanted. There was no phase at which Eupatridus could best her. The trickster ensured everything played out exactly as she wanted it.
As her overdramatic bragging session crescendoed, Eupatridus couldn’t help but laugh. He really was hopelessly outmatched.
“You now fully appreciate your situation?” Kat asked from on high.
“Yes, you’ve made it quite clear that I have been thoroughly beaten. You’re as devious as the good councilor who hosts many.”
“Thank you. That means a lot.”
“So, what now? You are right; you have my respect for outdoing me. Is my life forfeit.”
“Nope! Now we move to the next phase. In it I hope you will continue to call me councilor: trusting and executing my plans for everyone’s benefit.” Kat hopped down from his throne and shook his hand.
As she prepared to make her exit, she paused and turned back to him.
“Sorry for how I acted back there. It was just to get you on my side, honest. I didn’t mean to seem so smug and scary.” She nervously laughed.
“…sure?” Eupatridus was at a loss.
What a strange girl. They just talked but for some reason everything felt different now. Perhaps that was also part of her strange plans. Thus, Eupatridus who once called Kat “councilor” mockingly now genuinely called her his Councilor.

Notes:
-	“The good counsel” and “one who hosts many” are both epithets/ titles of Hades the god of the dead whose name was avoided at all costs by the ancient Greeks who thought uttering it directly was terrifying
