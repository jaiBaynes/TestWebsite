*Myth ?.5: Welcome to Olympia

PART 1
“We sing to Demeter, the holy goddess with the beautiful hair, she of the golden double-axe, she who glories in the harvest!” many men chanted in unison in the temple of Demeter, goddess of grain.
The little men were reminded of their place before the goddess’ glorious monument. At the dawn of day, they raised their hands up to the heavens where she dwelled and repeated after the priest who led them in the long and detailed prayer. When the ritual was completed and donations paid to the temple, the participants were ushered into the temple complex’s inner areas to receive some of its grain.
As beautiful as it was, this pristine temple was only one of her lesser “divine dwellings.” This “modest monument” was located outside the mortal city of Olympia, at the entrance to the great agricultural lands owned by the Cult of Demeter. As goddess of grain, bread, and the harvest, Demeter owned vast Megalifarma: great farms whose sacred bounty was closely guarded. Its supernatural produce grew far faster than natural crops and yielded multiple harvests a year.
Many who attended the cult ceremony and offered her praises and donations had come hoping for doles of grain and flour to avoid starvation. But before any of the great unwashed could receive their leftovers, the greater powers took as they pleased.
First, the Cult of Demeter set aside the best grain for its subsidiaries: aligned bakeries and markets. Then, grain for large investors who would use it for their own competing purposes, so long as their competition still paid tribute to the goddess. After that, the average bakeries receive their portion, with the quality of grain allotted by donation size to the temple.
Kasious, one such humble baker, waited for whatever scraps of grain he could bring home to his family business in hopes of keeping it afloat even a day longer. He watched the wagons full of good grain being escorted to his larger competitors, dreaming of a day when he could have access to such stock. All Graiac businesses fantasized about when they would be first in line.
But before he could thrive, he needed to survive. As a 16-year-old boy taking over his parents’ bakery after their deaths, many looked down on him. His standing was not helped by the lingering debt those parents left. Every entrepreneur’s dream of being uplifted by a beneficent daemon investor seemed less and less likely as the boy was left to stew in his predicament. The alternative and much more likely outcome was much more harrowing, especially considering…
Kasious, the young baker, was given pause as he watched the slaves being worked to the bone in the fields picking grain just beyond the enclosing fence. With each whip, various slurs were bellowed, reminding the slaves of their inferiority. Demi-humans had long been the majority stock of “agricultural workers” on Graiac plantations. Just as men used beasts of the field to till the soil, it seemed only fitting to make the subhumans with animal features pick the crops. Though humanoid in shape, their animalistic ears, tails, or limbs earned them much hatred by the higher race: humans who were proud to alone be made in the image of the gods. Many looked at the demi-human abuse with either indifference or satisfaction. But as Kasious watched, his Phrygian cap felt uncomfortable.
The wait for his grain felt like an eternity. Kasious occupied himself by inspecting the handful of “sacred grain” he had received as a gift after his “generous donation” to the temple meant to maintain his bakery’s status as a cult-approved establishment.
To make an average loaf, one would need about one pound of flour, equivalent to one pound of wheat. That was 13 square feet of wheat to produce one loaf of bread. As he did the mental math, he had to wonder: what was he meant to do with this handful? Display it until it rotted?
With a sigh, the poor baker turned to watch his wealthier competitors cut in line for their wagon-fulls of prized grain from Demeter’s Temple. Kasious had been “taught his place” and so fought his sense of jealousy. That was just the way of Olympia.
He turned to the great mountain: the city’s namesake. Blessed Mount Olympus towered in the distance, upon which the great gods supposedly made their abode. Was it selfish, Kasious wondered, to ponder why the gods with all their great powers did nothing for people like him who suffered in their shadow? No, that kind of thinking was dangerous. Everyone in Olympia was brought up to shun it! He had to pull himself up by his sandal straps that he might one day earn their favor and escape his dire circumstances. There was no miracle coming to save him; he had to stop holding out hope for one.

PART 2
“Hey there.” A white-haired girl with animal ears interrupted his thoughts.
Kasious panicked; where did she come from? The girl wore strange clothes; unlike anything he’d seen before. Whatever weird style it was, all Kasious could tell was that it was fancy: fine fabrics decorated with glittering golden trinkets and all manner of precious stone the ground could offer. Perhaps she was a new high-ranking priestess at the temple? But demi-humans were barred from priestly office. How did one acquire such wealth in segregated Graia?
“Sorry, I didn’t mean to startle you. My name is Kat and I’m new in town.” The girl held her hand out to shake.
“I see. It is a pleasure to meet you, my lady. I am Kasious the baker.” He shook her hand nervously, hoping he’d gotten the class-based honorifics correct.
Oh no; was he even allowed to shake her hand!? The upper class had so many unspoken rules they abided by; was her father going to have him executed!?
“Nice to meet’cha.” Kat didn’t seem to mind, “Would you happen to know where the informal settlements of Olympia’s underserved neighborhood are?”
Kasious froze up. Those were a lot of big words in a row, and he never went to school. Great! Now he was going to look like an idiot in front of the cute rich girl. Seeing his shy response, Kat rephrased her question.
“Hm… I believe that around here the area I’m looking for is called the ‘slums.’ Is that right?”
“Oh, the slums! I know where that is.”
“Amazing! I couldn’t find them on any map.”
“Of course it’s not on the map; the city likes to pretend we don’t exist. Besides, why would anyone go to the slums intentionally?”
“You’re from there?”
Kasious was too ashamed to answer. He was humiliating himself in front of such a high-class lady; was she mocking him?
“The city of Olympia is inside the great walls over there.” Kasious pointed towards the distant city, “We’re in the agricultural lands that surround the city for many miles. However, if you keep going in any direction away from town, you’ll eventually arrive at a second wall which encloses the farms. Past that is where you find the slums: the forgotten waste for people who can’t afford the protection of the walls.”
“All those people, left outside the walls’ protection?” the girl looked horrified.
“I’d recommend staying away. It’s dangerous out there for you.” Kasious knew such a wealthy target would be immediately set upon.
“No need to worry about me, I’ll be fine.” She smiled, “I have Gatola to protect me.”
It took Kasious a moment to realise she was motioning towards a tiny, adorable creature that stood by her side. Was it some kind of exotic weasel? Even the girl’s pet was adorned with more wealth than Kasious would ever acquire. Yeah, she’d be a sitting duck in the slums.
“Again, I really would advise against going…” Kasious nervously laughed.
“When you’re done with your business here, I’d love to meet up out there.” Kat shrugged off his warning.
Kasious sighed; obviously a peasant like him had no right to command a superior in rank.
“I’m creating a report on Olympia, and I’d love to hear about your experiences as a citizen of this town.” Kat beamed.
“Citizen, funny joke.” Kasious laughed.
In Olympia like other Graiac poleis, the title of “citizen” was a coveted privilege for a select few wealthy land-owning men who populated the inner city right next to Mount Olympus itself. Lowly scum like Kasious would be thrown in jail for pretending to hold the distinguished rank of “citizen.” This girl definitely wasn’t from around here.
“I’d be happy to help however I can my lady. And if you really do go to the slums, you should stop by my bakery.” Kasious gave in and remembered to always be advertising, “Hellen Bakery is always bound to brighten your day. I’ll be opening up shop once my shipment of grain arrives.”
“Cute pun. I’ll be sure to stop by. See you around Kasious.” Kat waved before heading off.

PART 3
What a nice lady. Kasious was left to ponder her strange arrival: confidently wandering around without a male guardian and flaunting her fancy clothes so fearlessly. If she could spread the word of his bakery to her rich friends maybe he could turn things around! His grain was still taking an eternity to arrive, but Kasious happily passed the time fantasizing about how he could finally escape destitution using this opportunity.
“What are you smirking about, boy? Plotting mischief?” a high-ranking priest demanded as he passed by.
“Of course not, Lord Dissognắthos!” Kasious fell down, bowing before the priest.
Priest Dissognắthos was the high priest of this temple who oversaw the allocation of all Demeter’s grain in this part of Olympia. He was her worldly representative in the world of men who handled her worldly affairs. Why did he have time to be wandering the temple grounds?
“You are addressing a servant of the gods, baker. Why is that cap still on.” The priest expected it removed as a common sign of deference to superiors.
“My Lord, I mean no disrespect…” Kasious hesitated, earning the priest’s ire.
Ultimately, he could not bring himself to remove it and so tried to change the subject by saying, “You led a wonderful service today, sir. I am always so inspired by your devotion to our golden lady.”
“I see.” The priest replied as he noticed the boy’s grain had finally arrived.
“Send it back.” He stopped the servants bringing out the wagons, “For slighting a priest of Demeter he shall receive the last and the worst of our crop. And mark him down… his store may be due for inspection.”
Kasious was horrified. This was the power of Graia’s elites: even minor perceived slights could be repaid by life ruining consequences. Unlike the girl, the priest acted his wealth. Kasious left Demeter’s temple dejected. He could tell just from looking at the grain it was no good. But perhaps it was befitting his station.

PART 4
Following the directions he’d previously given Kat, Kasious ventured outside of the agricultural lands and past the great defensive walls which enclosed them. There he saw the slums where the poorest of the poor who could not afford the enclosed protection subsisted. After passing through the hefty toll booth, he was back home, the run-down refuse in the shadow of the great metropolis. How could things get any worse?
“Let me go!” a young girl screamed as two ruffians grabbed her, “I’ve got a knife!”
As soon as he arrived home, Kasious panicked: the loan sharks were harassing his little sister Glyka!
“Oh dear!” the thugs laughed at her dull bread-knife threat, “Aren’t you a little young to be armed, miss?”
“Aren’t you a little old to be asking stupid questions!?” she swung the bread knife at them as well as her little arms could.
“Let her go!” Kasious rushed to her aid.
“There you are boy. Late to arrive and late on your payments.” The thug who wasn’t holding his sister grinned.
“I… I said I’ll have your money soon. It’s just been a slow month. The big bakery that opened up down the street has-” Kasious began.
“Save your excuses. The Don is all that keeps us safe outside the safety of the walls. Pay up or you will no longer be protected.” The thug glared at Kasious, “And by the way, little girl…”
He turned around and drew a rusted, bloodstained dagger, “THIS is what a real knife looks like.”
Kasious froze with horror as the goon moved to stab it into the counter by the girl to make his point.
“Excuse me.” Kat interrupted after appearing seemingly from nowhere!
Her sudden arrival stunned everyone, breaking up the tense scene.
“Let her go, please.” Kat smiled at him.
The thug did so, though not altruistically. A far better prize had just presented itself. They immediately spied the expensive clothing and jewelry Kat wore. As soon as his hand was off Kasious’ sister, the thug reached for her but even faster, Kat had grabbed his hand.
“It isn’t nice to try and touch people without permission.” She calmly replied.
Everyone saw it or rather didn’t; the moment between when her hand was down and when she stopped him. Was she some kind of witch?
“Then unhand me!” he swung his knife at her arm.
His sharpened, killer’s knife did even less damage to her than the little girl’s dull bread knife did to him. The blade didn’t even leave a scratch on Kat. What it did do was shatter on contact with her skin leaving all the witnesses utterly baffled.
“Sorry about that. The knife didn’t hold any sentimental value, did it?” she asked, completely unphased.
Nobody could react to this insane moment.
“I came to this town to see it for myself, and I am utterly distraught by what I’ve found. Guys like you… play a large part in the people’s plight, don’t you?” Kat demanded.
The girl was far from intimidating. Her gentle scold and finger wag would’ve been seen as adorable moreso than scary under normal circumstances. Yet the two goons ran off in utter terror. Kasious was amazed. This mysterious girl wasn’t a new priestess but with such strange airs around her was there a chance she was…

Notes:
-	Graia is a fantasy equivalent of ancient Greece with many aspects from mythology. It is based on the Roman name for Greece: Greaca. Like the name Graia has various distortions and differences from Greece
-	The prayer performed at the start of the chapter to Demeter is based on actual ancient Greek prayers according to Homer. In addition to ancient prayer rituals, the verbiage itself is a reference to the beginning of the Homeric Hymn to Demeter as translated by Gregory Nagy. Here is a cute comic illustrating more of the specifics: https://www.instagram.com/p/DKJxAcvKxGm/?img_index=6&igsh=MTQ3dm0yemc3dmw2aA== 
-	Kasious’ name is a pun referencing the terrible video game Nekopara and it’s protagonist Kashou. I watched the Let’s Player’s at TeamFourStar Gaming play it on their show Yes! Yes! Love Adventure Go! Kasious is the Greek word for “cashew” which the let’s players assume is the main character’s namesake and intentionally pronounce it like that.
-	Glyka, Kasious’ little sister, has a similar name pun. Her name means “sweet” in ancient Greek just as Kashou’s sister Shigure likely has a name pun for “sugary.” The Let’s Players made many jokes about the character so she is much more memorable to me than she should be.
-	Priest Dissognắthos’ name is a pun meaning “Priest Doublechin” in ancient Greek. This is a reference to the French term “pretre de double-menton” meaning the “priest of the double chin.” I heard and noted this term after watching  Michael Parenti’s “The Political Uses of Religion” Denver, Colorado 4.18.87  where he describes the political uses of religion where the aforementioned priests line their pockets while exploiting the poor as opposed to liberation Theologians who actually care about people in need.
-	Olympia is a human city on earth beneath the floating city of the gods called Olympus. In real life the city of Olympia was a small town near Mount Olympus. In this fantasy setting, Olympia is a massive metropolis enriched by proximity to the gods.
-	Megalifarma is an invented term roughly translating to “Great Farm” in Greek. It is a Greek equivalent to the real-world historical term Latifundia: Roman plantations roughly translating to “spacious farm”
-	The term “daemon investor” is reference to the modern concept of “angel investor.” The change in name is a pun based on the fact that the ancient Greeks religion didn’t believe in angels so the closest term would be “daemon” a supernatural spirit that is close enough to modern concepts of “angels” despite sounding closer to “demons.”
-	The use of “citizen” here is somewhat anachronistic. The definition provided is based on what citizen meant in ancient Athens (called “polítēs” in Greek). However, during this story’s time period (the bronze age) that wouldn’t exist yet. A more accurate term would be “laoi” meaning “people under arms” and described in Homer’s Iliad.
-	Hellen Bakery (on top of continuing the reference to Yes Yes Love Adventure Go’s Let’s Play) is a pun that both means “Greek” and “bright” or “light”
-	Kat Trivia: One of Kat’s sets of elaborate gold earrings include functioning bells. Strangely they make no sound when she moves.
