*Myth ?.513: Eupatridus

PART 1
“Do you know why I became tyrant? THE Tyrant of this town? Before there were any pretenders to my title; why was I the undisputed champion?” Eupatridus asked a restrained, terrified man.
His answer was prompted by literally twisting his arm.
“Because anyone who got in your way is dead!”
“And yet you were stupid enough to steal from me.”
“I didn’t know it was your money! I swear!”
“This ticket all the way to Athens says otherwise.” The tyrant threw the papers onto the floor.
The accused had nothing to say.
“Nobody enters or leaves this town without my knowledge. And nobody moves one obol in my territory without my knowledge either. Everyone knows that. You knew that and yet you still tried to steal from me. It is not just about the money it is your audacity that appalls me. A low-level runt trying to make a mockery of me.”
One of Eupatridus’ men entered with one of their gang’s signature daggers, ready to execute him on the spot. The restrained man began cackling insanely as his inevitable death shattered his mind.
“Understand, I have an image to protect.” Eupatridus declared. 
“Oh yes, an image on the outside but a very different one for your sweet wife, isn’t that right!” the man tried to spit at the tyrant.
Enraged, Eupatridus stood up and seized the knife, ready to slay him himself! However, just before he stabbed him, the door burst open.
“Who told you to enter?” Eupatridus pointed the knife in a rage.
“The Lady has returned, great Tyrant. I have reported as you commanded me.” The terrified servant squeaked.
Eupatridus paused. He set down the knife, without a moment to lose. The terrifying tyrant immediately rushed out the door, stopping only to check the mirror and ensure he had no blood on him. From fuming and ready to kill, he practically danced his way through the halls. Past the various statues and frescoes of his family and especially his darling wife, Eupatridus seemed like a completely different man.
The light of his life; the reason he did all this was finally back. But he couldn’t be too happy; though she frequently went to the temple her illness only seemed to worsen, that was why the Tyrant decided to spend every moment he could spending time with her. But he held faith: that healing priest he’d spent a fortune on bringing in would arrive before it was too late. There was nothing he wouldn’t do for her.
That was why he was brought to tears when he saw that his wife was fully cured of her sickness.
“Praise to the gods! I must donate to Lord Apollo most generously for his gift!” he hugged his wife, crying tears of joy.
The loyal soldiers of the Tyrant all turned away, knowing better than to observe their master crying.
“We can talk about that later. Everybody else out, now!” she commanded.
Pikrosa had some strong words for Eupatridus about what she saw in town. This was a moment he always dreaded; he’d do anything to give her the life she always wanted. It was the life she deserved… he just didn’t want her to see where it came from.
“Everything I’ve done, I’ve done for you.” Eupatridus insisted.
“Don’t you dare say that. After what we suffered out there, under the thumb of crimelords, you’ve become just like them!”
“No, I am nothing like those two-bit crooks crawling out of every alleyway. I run this town. I brought order; united people and ended the chaos! I am a job’s provider!”
“Dear, the glory days are over. You are no longer the only tyrant. Whatever you think you built seems to have almost immediately collapsed back into feuding crimebosses. Or are you still going to insist you’re nothing like the other three villains ruining this town oh good tyrant?”
“It is adapt or die. I’m sorry my dear but the dirtiest games are also the most essential in maintaining power. Even still, my title is not for show. Whatever you saw out there is much better than the brutes encroaching on our territory. My necessary evils keep true depravity far from you and ensures you live in the lap of luxury!”
“Unbelievable. What a fool I was. I always dismissed the stories of destitution I heard, being the result of how the slums naturally are. To think as we grew richer it was off the backs of those people that’s we’d gone from exploited to exploiters! All those honest business ventures in Olympia are fueled by your cruelest crimes in the slums!”
“My wife; my love… would you prefer to be back out there suffering as we once did?”
Pikrosa glared at him. Eupatridus realized he’d messed up.
“You’re sleeping on the couch tonight.” She commanded, “And tomorrow you shall meet and reward the darling girl who saved my life.”
“…yes dear.” Eupatridus groaned. 

PART 2
The cat was out of the bag. After years of molding his façade as the good tyrant to clear his wife’s conscience (and his own) it would take a lot of work to repair this damage. Perhaps meeting his beloved’s savior would uplift his spirits.
“Oh cool! So, they actually do have apples here!” the mysterious girl his wife brought the next day cheered as she inspected the bowl of fruit. This strange child was his savior?
“That is meant for decoration.” Eupatridus sighed.
“But it’s real fruit.” Kat inspected it and confirmed, “Seems like a waste to leave them out as props to be thrown out when they spoiled. Most people in the slums would literally kill for fresh fruit.”
That comment earned Eupatridus a glare from Pikrosa.
“Be that as it may, I am willing to offer you much more than some fruit. My wife is my everything. I am the good Tyrant Eupatridus; as the gods command, on my honor as a man, I repay everyone in kind. For saving her life, a payment of one million drachmas is a meager starting offer.” Eupatridus declared.
“No thanks. I love teaching people. Giving your wife a crash course on microbiology was fun enough to count as payment, right?” Kat asked.
“I insist on a real payment. The good tyrant is known for his generosity. Does ten million drachmas approach the number you want to hear?”
“Why are you so insistent on giving me money? Do you really have that much to waste?”
Eupatridus flinched as his wife’s glare intensified.
“If you offered something better, I would be all ears.”
“Hm… if I really had to get a reward… could you allow me access to your organization. I’d love to get the details for my report.”
“Report? You work for another tyrant? Or the Judges perhaps?” Eupatridus grew tense.
“Nope. Neither of them. My motives are pure; just want to learn about how the organization of Tyrant Eupatridus works.”
Eupatridus hesitated but given his wife’s glares he indulged, “If you were given access… you wouldn’t tell a soul, correct?”
“I don’t know dear; I’d be quite interested to learn more about your operations. There seems to be so much to discover.” Pikrosa passive-aggressively chimed in,
“None other than my wife would see it?” Eupatridus groaned on thin ice.
“Okie dokey.” Kat gave a thumbs up, “Also, if I have any suggestions for you, can I have regular audiences to report back?”
“Suggestions?”
“Think of it like an audit, it would be good practice for my future career plans.”
Eupatridus was at a loss for words.
“That sounds wonderful, doesn’t it dear?” Pikrosa smiled.
“Very well. Do as you please.” Eupatridus sighed.
“Awesome! I’ll be sure to give you daily updates, Rosa.” Kat smiled.
Rosa? Was that supposed to be a cutesy nickname for the empress of Eupatridus’ empire!? Where did this child… never mind. If the missus liked her, he would let it slide.
“Just don’t get in my way.” Eupatridus settled on his response.
“I wouldn’t dream of it! Helping people out is what I’m all about! I’ll be sure to make myself useful around here.” Kat beamed.

PART 3
So, it was. For a week, the Eupatridus palace had a strange girl poking around, bothering all the employees. She poked through files, recorded the layout of the building itself and even shadowed personnel during their daily activities. Knowing that all she found would be reported back to the lady of the house, things were run a bit differently than usual. Everybody was on their best behavior, trying to keep things squeaky clean.
“It’s funny, you’ve gotten a lot more popular around town lately.” Kat noted.
“How so?” Eupatridus asked during their first weekly counseling session.
“Ever since I’ve been shadowing your men, whenever I interview people around town they report a huge improvement in interactions. Ever since they realized I interviewed people they interacted with they’ve been on their best behavior even when I’m not around.” Kat laughed.
“How nice.”
“Since nobody was shaken down for protection fees, there are rumors the good tyrant cancelled them all. How generous.”
Eupatridus was not amused. If that’s what this naïve girl was plotting, she was in for a shock when he ratcheted up future fees to make up for lost profits. He had a crime empire to run, and this girl was proving much more costly than if he just paid her an exorbitant fee up front.
“How long are you going to be doing this for?” Eupatridus groaned.
“Dunno. How about you?”
“Excuse me?”
“The tyrant thing, how much longer are you going to do it?”
“As long as I can provide for my family, I will. By the grace of the gods.”
“You haven’t thought about what happens after?”
“I enjoy a blissful rest the flowers in the Elysian fields of the hereafter.” Eupatridus chuckled. Who was he kidding. After everything he did in life the only part of the Underworld he was destined for was Tartarus.
“I was suggesting retirement, not death. But since you brought it up, do you have a will and stuff worked out.”
“Yes, but I’m not adding you to it. You wasted your reward I’m afraid.”
“That’s okay. What might be a problem though is how central you are to your empire. With you gone, it’d probably follow shortly after.”
Eupatridus furrowed his brow, “I run a tight ship. It is sure to survive.”
“You do indeed. You keep a great number of problems at bay quite well.”
“Thank you.”
“And yet many of the issues giving you headaches now are assured fault lines for your enterprise that might shatter it all the second you leave. I was hoping I could give you a heads up on those.”
“Like what?”
“The slums are getting bigger.”
“I know. More people to protect, but more people to… return my generosity. It’s a net benefit.”
“The population size is increasing yeah. But more importantly, I mean it is physically getting larger.”
“And?”
“Well, after going through some of your records I confirmed a hypothesis. Your greatest losses of territory and the rise of your competitors ending your monopoly correlate very well with the growth of the slums. Every time your territory expanded, your control over all of it diminished.”
“Is that so?”
“Yep. All 3 of the other tyrants’ rise closely lag large increases to the slum’s size.”
“And you’re saying as things grow, my influence will shrink.”
“Exactly! You are an effective administrator; I’ll give you that. But you’re only one man. Out of fear of giving your subordinates too much power, you’ve made yourself central to more processes than you can reasonably handle. Naturally your fears materialize: people lower in the ranks capitalize on your blind spots and enrich themselves. One of your current rivals rose to power exactly that way.”
“Antinouous… you’re saying there may be more snakes like him in the future?”
“Even if they don’t break away like he did, it’s almost as harmful if they stay in your camp. Opportunists skimming money off the top without sufficient oversight is bound to eat into your profits. You have money everywhere, they knew you won’t notice if some of it goes missing.”
“I have noticed and have been… dealing with it.”
“You sure?”
Eupatridus was given pause by Kat’s sudden grave expression.
“There once was a king of Sicily who won a great empire in life. But winning it proved much easier than maintaining it. He could expand no farther because the strife within what he already had became too great. But he was wise and clever, so it endured as long as he lived. However, at his death his kingdom fell to ruin. He had no viable successor to rule as wisely as he did. His generals fought amongst each other for power and his neighboring competitors invaded his lands to capitalize on the chaos. His poor family, as political threats to the new would-be kings were horribly killed in the chaos.” Kat declared.
“You are not being very subtle, girl.” Eupatridus sat back in his chair.
“That really happened in the past. It is a known weakness of successful kingdoms. The Titans of old fell in much the same way; treachery from within paired with ambitious rivals. I’m just saying, if it can happen to them shouldn’t you be worried too?”
“And what would you have me do, oh councilor?” Eupatridus rolled his eyes.
“Where I’m from, people care a lot about leaving the world better than they found it. They ensure they have a good legacy to leave for those who remain.”
“Do you think I enjoy doing what must be done? It’s too late for me to walk away. There is no other way.”
“Don’t say that yet.”

Notes:
-	The Tyrant’s name Eupatridus Archos is a rough Greek equivalent of the name Alphonse Capone which both mean “well-born leader” in English. Al Capone was a famous real-world gangster.
-	His title as the “Good Tyrant” is my jab at “Good King” characters and tropes. Putting good in front of any other form of dictatorial position reveals how silly it is on its face. The“Good dictator” or the “Good autocrat” sounds obviously absurd, but for some reason tons of people think “Good king sounds any better.”
-	The Tyrants of Olympia are akin to mob bosses. In ancient Greece calling someone a tyrant was not an insult for a person in position of power. To the ancient Greeks, a tyrant was simply a ruler who seized power unconstitutionally, usually through military force, rather than through traditional means like inheritance or election. The term "tyrant" did not have the negative connotations it carries today; it simply referred to someone who ruled with absolute authority, often autocratic, and often without a clear legal basis for their rule.
-	The double meaning of ancient vs modern definitions is to put readers in my shoes: people saying “Good tyrant” sounds absurd to modern readers while an ancient person would say it in the same way people today might say “good king” without realizing how strange that sounds to other people
-	Kat Trivia: Kat’s favorite fruit is pomegranate and she was somewhat disappointed that even Eupatridus couldn’t get his hands on any of the exotic, prohibitively expensive fruit.
