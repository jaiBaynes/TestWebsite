*Myth ?.519: Antinous

Antinous was the newest tyrant who had carved his territory out of the eldest’s. Once his trusted second in command, after revolting from his mentor Antinous was sure to keep his spies in place throughout Eupatridus’ organization.
Many of them had been grumbling about how radically Eupatridus had changed ever since a mysterious arrival appeared. Changes? Suggestions? Since when did the tyrant take advice from anyone? To protect his image, it was a household secret the Tyrant was increasingly coming under the influence of a little girl.
After the first week’s meeting he was raving about her absurdity. After the second, he was receiving second opinions from others. On the third he was implementing her ideas after approval from his closest allies. After a month, she was essentially writing policy for the organization.
“So that’s who has chosen to replace me?” Antinous cackled at the news, “Tell him I would like to meet. It’s been a while since I’ve paid the old man a visit.”
So it was; the next day Antinous and his armed guards marched up to Eupatridus’ palace where they were greeted by his own forces. The soldiers waited by the front as Antinous entered alone, confidently striding through the palace of his rival which had been emptied for their meeting. However, he found Theódoros hastily lounging outside the palace.
“Hey there brat.” Antinous approached the boy he hated.
Antinous’ contempt for the child was natural. After all, back when Eupatridus had no heirs, everyone assumed Antinous would be adopted into the family and succeed him. That boys birth ruined everything and was why he had to go his own way. He stole what Antinous had his eyes on for years; he worked faithfully as Eupatridus’ second in command for so long and was getting his throne no matter what.
If this sniveling brat was his main rival in the battle for Eupatridus’ throne, he’d practically won already. Once the old fart finally kicked the bucket, Antinous was poised to take what was rightfully his.
“I thought you were here to see my father…” Theo nervously backed away from his old bully.
“I’m here to catch up. I heard you’re finally pursuing your thespian dreams. Land any roles yet?” Antinous’ question was dripping with contempt. His feigned interest served only to highlight the absurdity of a prominent noble of the slums lowering himself to the menial position of actor.
“Did you finally learn to communicate with your father? Or in his senile years did he forget the consequences of your foolishness being left unchecked?”
“Will you give it a rest!? I never wanted my father’s role. I didn’t mean to take it from you. I wasn’t planning on being the next tyrant.”
“Don’t worry. You won’t be.” Antinous smiled, terrifying the boy who got up and left.
Content with bully the boy enough for one day, Antinous headed to Eupatridus’ room and pulled up a chair of his own. The room was full of a tense silence as the two tyrants met face to face.
“It’s quite nostalgic being back here, old man. It all seems so familiar… and yet I’ve heard strange rumors making it seem like everything has changed.” Antinous began.
“Oh really?” Eupatridus raised an eyebrow.
“I hear you have your best torturers doing milk runs and your toughest thugs helping old ladies take out their garbage.” Antinous laughed, “But now it all makes sense.”
“How so?”
“I cracked the code with the debt slavery goldmine and this is your response? I’d be so insulted if I wasn’t so intrigued… why is it working? Is it working? Because mobs in my territory are grumbling that we don’t have what the good tyrant does.” Antinous got very close.
Eupatridus had no reply.
“They have no more protection fees in Eupatridus’ territory. They have no sickness in Eupatridus’ territory. They have free bread in Eupatridus’ territory. On and on their rambling goes. You’re trying to make me look bad, is that it?”
“I am governing in a way that most benefits my domain. Not everything is about you, boy.”
“Right, nothing’s about me. This is to safeguard your legacy to protect your little son, right? When you’re dead people won’t get vengeance on him because they’ll love you so much.”
“That thought crossed my mind. But it is far from the whole story.”
“I’m here seeking your wisdom, my old mentor. Enlighten me; tell me if it is senior wisdom or senile folly that guides you.”
“It is neither.”
“Then these changes are not in you… but in that mysterious councilor you have recently acquired.”
“And there it is. All you pretenders to my empire fight against me, but as soon as I gain an advantage you crawl back asking for the secret to my success. It’s pathetic.” Eupatridus laughed.
“The others have visited you too?”
“Not visit here but we’ve been in touch. What? Your spies didn’t tell you?”
Antinous flinched.
“I know full well that the complaints you mentioned before are far more dire than you let on. Many of your people have been flooding here, pledging their support to me. You’re in no position to punish them with your resources spread so thin; plagued by the same problems I faced before the councilor came to my aid.”
“You’re suggesting that your foolishness is actually the secret to your success?”
“I am suggesting that you finally learn patience. Stew on your questions until the next meeting of the Tyrants in a few days. My Councilor shall be making her appearance then.”
“Very well…” Antinous left angrily.
How annoying, that didn’t go at all how he planned. He needed to blow off some steam. Since he could not dominate the wily old fox, he would look for his useless kit to torment. Instead of exiting immediately, he searched for where Theo had moved to on the palace grounds.
He couldn’t find the boy but rather came across a beautiful young woman with cat ears. Adorned in wealth and splendor, gorgeous demi-human girl… she was the mysterious arrival all the rumors were about, wasn’t she.
“Ah, you must be Eupatridus’ mysterious councilor.” Antinous approached the girl, “He didn’t mention you were so fetching.”
“My appearance is irrelevant when discussing my activities here, isn’t it?” Kat smiled.
“Perhaps. I always wondered how such a guarded man would let anyone into his court. Though it makes much more sense you were able to sleep your way into the position now. No wonder he eagerly increased the frequence of your private sessions.”
“Please don’t insinuate those kinds of things about Eupatridus. Assaulting his character like that is uncalled for.” Kat grinned.
“You sure it’s his reputation you don’t want getting out there? Or is it just that you prefer to get the drop on your targets.”
“Me? I don’t care what people say about me. I’ve been called far worse since I got here. Plenty of new slurs I could add to my report at least.”
“Why not join me then? A great many demmi-humans are under my protection.”
“I wouldn’t call your racial policy protection. You just knew which population to target.”
“Eupatridus told you about me?”
“Your people told me about you.”
“All good things I hope.”
“I mean, it gave me a better impression of you than the one I’m getting now.”
Antinous lost his fake smile.
“Eupatridus may baby you, girl but don’t forget that you’re speaking to a tyrant. I am next to a king around here.”
“Darn, so close to giving me blackmail material on him too.” Kat spoke not to him, but the strange pet she balanced on her lap.
“Listen here, girl! You will show me respect, or I will have to teach it to you!” Antinous reached to touch Kat.
The second his hand got too close, the unassuming kitten she held erupted into a massive otherworldly beast, roaring and snarling at him! Antinous screamed, falling back as the beast prepared to maul him for approaching its master.
“Down Gatola. He’s not a threat.” Kat didn’t even have to look.
Antinous opened his eyes to find it was back to being a defenseless critter in the girl’s hands. He stumbled away trembling in the face of this demon.
“You’re a monster! What was that!?” he stammered.
“It’s interesting. Plenty of people have tried to mug me or attack me since I got here. Out of desperation, out of misdirection, but Gatola never reacted so aggressively to them. You really are irredeemable, aren’t you?” Kat’s piercing eyes locked onto him.
Antinous had no words, he could only tremble there as Kat stood up and walked past him. When he turned around she was gone. What kind of horrible monster had Eupatridus has unwittingly brought into their midst.

Notes:
-	The tyrant Antinous is named after the leader of the villainous suitors in Homer’s Odyssey. Like Homer’s character, this Antinous is a brute trying to seize power from a ruler 
-	Kat Trivia: Kat chose Gatola’s beast form to be based on the Cave Lion and extinct pre-historic relative of modern lions.
