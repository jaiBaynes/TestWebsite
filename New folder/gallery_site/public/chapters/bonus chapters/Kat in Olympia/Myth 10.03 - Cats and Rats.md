Myth ?.511: Cats and Rats

PART 1
Glyka was a rat demi-human like her brother. Their parents told them it was nothing to be ashamed of… but it was something to hide.
“It wouldn’t do to have a chef be the embodiment of unclean animals. Who would eat what we made then?” Kasious had told Kat.
Rats were vermin; even the demi-humans with their features hated them. One of the bakers’ greatest concerns was keeping them out of their supplies. In Graia, various people owned weasels: pets kept around specifically to eat rats.
A bad person was called a rat, to “rat” was to betray; on and on it went. That was why even from a young age, Glyka got the message Graiac society was trying to send. Demi-humans were scum and rat demi-humans were the lowest of the low.
That was why Kat’s treatment of them was so refreshing. When nobody else was around, Kasious was willing to show Kat his extra set of ears. Following his example, Glyka did as well, wearing them without shame when she was around.
Kat had all sorts of interesting tips and suggestions: ways one’s animal ears could be kept comfortable or even decorated. In Kat’s homeland where demi-humans seemed to exist without fear there were even special hats and hairstyles one could wear to show off their animal features with pride.
“Kat, what kind of demi-human are you?” Glyka asked.
“My name doesn’t give it away?” Kat asked.
“…no?” the clueless girl replied.
“I’m a cat; that’s why my papa named me that.”
“…what’s a cat?”
“What do you mean?”
“What’s a cat?”
Kat and Glyka looked at each other, equally baffled by the other.
“Little creatures kept as pets.” Kat mimed stereotypical cat behavior. “Used to be for catching pests.”
“Like a weasel?”
“Kasious? Do you seriously not have cats around here?” Kat called out.
“What’s a cat?” Kasious poked his head in the room.
“You know lions and panthers, right?”
“Yeah.”
“So, you do know what cats are.”
“…I know what lions and panthers are.”
“They’re cats.”
“They are?”
“But you said people keep them as pets. Nobody can keep a lion as a pet.” Glyka butted in.
“Gatola is a lion.” Kat motioned towards her own pet, “Well in this form, he’s a lion cub. Kind of…”
“Looks more like a weasel.” Kasious and Glyka replied in synchronized confusion.
“Fascinating!” Kat dashed from doing Glyka’s hair to write nothing down on her mysterious metal tablet.

PART 2
From there it was decided, Kat’s mission for the day was to find even a single cat in the slums to show Glyka. Wherever they went all pet owners seemed to only have weasels for pest control or dogs for hunting. There weren’t even any stray cats!
When they couldn’t find any in Kasious’ neighborhood they went out to the Aegyptian-town section of the slums. Failed foreign merchants, stranded travelers and more had slowly populated various collections of forgotten ethnic poor in the slums. In Graia, demi-humans were the lowest of the low, but they all but shared that spot with foreigners. Xenophobic Graiacs segregated even the slums: the poorest Graiac held their head high with dignity that at least they weren’t a foreigner. They were too distracted by that to notice the richer “fellow Graiacs” were picking their pocket.
However, Kat was friendly towards them and even spoke their language. Glyka couldn’t understand what she was saying, but after conversing with a man she led Glyka to their next stop.
“That’s a cat.” Kat pointed at an Aegyptian Mau cat lounging in a yard.
“No that’s a weasel.” Glyka looked at Kat in confusion.
“No that’s a weasel.” Kat pointed to an actual weasel owned by the cat owner’s neighbor.
“They’re different looking kinds of weasels but they’re both weasels. Like how there are different kinds of dogs.”
“But that housecat is closer to a wild cat than a weasel.”
“There are wild cats?”
“Yes, like lions.”
“But lions have manes. That cat doesn’t have a mane, so it looks more like a weasel.”
Kat looked to the Aegyptian cat owner for help and exchanged a few words in the foreign language.
Kat went over to pet the cat and asked it, “Do you think you look like a weasel?”
The cat meowed back making Kat laugh, “See. He agrees with me. He’s not a weasel.”
“Very funny.” Glyka rolled her eyes, “We get it, you can speak Aegyptian but that doesn’t mean you can speak weasel.”
“Do you think you look like a cat?” Kat asked the weasel, pointing to the cat.
The weasel trilled and Kat supposedly translated, “I look nothing like him. What are you talking about.”
“Just because I’m a kid doesn’t mean I fall for anything.” Glyka puffed up her cheeks in a pout at Kat’s perceived teasing.
The pet owners laughed, also thinking that Kat was just teasing the girl. Before leaving, Kat spoke some more with the people in the Aegyptian town. Glyka couldn’t understand them but could guess what she said the next day when many of them showed up in line for free bread. Not just them but other enclaves of foreign people gathered at Kasious’ bakery for bread as well.
The poor Graiacs were tense at first when the foreigners joined them. Many prejudiced thoughts, many directed towards the Graiac poor, were echoed from them to their neighbors simply for having a different race, religion and creed.
However, nobody wanted to cause a scene and risk not getting their free bread. Especially when they noticed some weren’t there to get bread but to help bake it. Was that why Kat and Glyka helped bring more ovens to bake yesterday?
“Emmer wheat bread! Ah, that authentic Aegyptian taste.” Kat made a big show enjoying the strange foreign bread the new bakers baked. She even gifted random Graiac people other types of bread in exchange for public shows of enjoyment. She could be sneaky when she wanted… but to what end?
Glyka’s confusion was compounded by the grumbling of the Graiacs in line. On top of hating outsiders being here they grumbled about now having to specify which type of bread they wanted. How outrageous! Why exactly? Glyka didn’t get why the grownups were so mad. She helped sample all the bread types for auditioning new bakers and thought all the types were tasty in their own way. Why would people be mad about having more choices?

PART 3
Oh well, Glyka didn’t have time to think about that. Kat had set up a play area where children could wait as their parents stood in line within view. Glyka’s job for the day was to be nice and play with the new kids so they could feel welcome. She took the mission; after all she got cool toys to show them in exchange.
It was fun. Some of them looked different but the kids were all just like her after all. Glyka could tell Kat was up to something but couldn’t exactly tell what. Then a demi-human girl Glyka’s age nervously approached, asking if she was really allowed to play with them all too. Then it clicked.
“Your ears are pretty Glyka, don’t let anyone shame you for them. One day… I’ll make sure you can wear them with pride; that you can be yourself and be accepted.” Kat smiled at her the other day.
Glyka eagerly invited the demi-human girl in, hoping she could help Kat so that one day she too could be accepted as she truly was. That evening, after the bakery closed for the day, Kat finally returned. After helping set up in the morning and stabilizing the tense situation with the new arrivals Kat had left to interview more people around town for her report.
Of course, Glyka eagerly told her all about the new friends she made that day. Kat added the girl’s testimony to her report after first asking her permission but that finally made her ask the burning question.
“Kat, what is this report thing you’re always working on?” Glyka asked.
“It’s the reason I came to Graia. People in my homeland like to have up to date knowledge on how things are in Olympia so they can prepare for the Olympics.”
“The Olympics? The big festival the hero Hercules is throwing next year!?” Glyka was stunned.
“Exactly. Since my city will be participating, they want to prepare properly before sending people here. Plus, the last report on Olympia is 10 years old so our information needs to be updated.”
“And a report will help with that?”
“Yeah. I write everything down and then pass it along to others. It’s kind of like a report for school.”
“What’s school?” Glyka asked.
Kat lost her smile. While she was amused by Graiacs not knowing about cats, this young girl being deprived of a proper education was saddening.
“School is a place where people go to safely learn. Where I come from, children go to school sort of like the play area you were in. It’s a spot parents can trust their children will be watched while they go work or do other activities.”
“So, like a private tutor? You really are a rich girl, aren’t you.”
“You’re close with the private tutor connection. Except you get to learn with all your friends. Instead of them going to a noble’s house in school all the children, no matter their family’s position gets to go.”
“Wild!”
“Yep, everyone gets to read and write. But I thought I saw you reading before… so I forgot you don’t have that in Graia.”
“My mama taught me how to read prices when we went shopping. Everybody learns from their parents that way. But that’s about it. If the numbers get too big it’s over for me.”
“I see. Quite worrying in a land obsessed with written contracts.”
“Silly Kat. Nobody actually reads contracts.”
“Doubly worrying.” Kat looked to the refurbished statue of Hermes which marked the simple marketplace where Kasious’ bakery was.
“Oh well. It’s grown-up problems anyways.” Glyka shrugged.
“What do you want to do when you grow up?”
“I dunno. I think we’re probably too poor for me to be worth marrying off. What else could I do? If I don’t get the plague and get to grow up, I hope I can figure it out.”
“Wow.” Kat laughed, “That’s a depressing answer.”
“Thanks.” Glyka had never heard that word before.
“And? Don’t you want to hear what I want to be when I grow up?”
“Aren’t you already a grown up?”
“Ouch! I’m only 15, that’s not that old.”
Glyka looked confused. In Graia childhood didn’t last long. The surprising thing about Kasious wasn’t that he worked a full-time job; it was that he ran the bakery. With an average life expectancy of 35 to 50 in Graia, Kasious and Kat were basically considered adults there. 
“I guess you aren’t an adult yet since you aren’t married yet. Hey wanna marry Kasious. You seem loaded and that way we could sponge off you!” Glyka beamed.
Kasious who was bringing snacks over, tripped over in a panic at that suggestion. Kat was instantly there to catch Kasious and the falling snacks so nothing broke.
“Thank you, Kat. Glyka stop saying strange things!” Kasious blushed uncontrollably.
“What? She’s the best thing that ever happened to us. Kasious take one for the team and let me marry you off!” Glyka protested.
“Don’t talk like that Glyka, people will think we’re just using her for her money!” Kasious panicked, “Which we aren’t by the way; I’m not a gold digger I swear!”
“I hope not ‘cause I’m just about broke now.” Kat laughed.
“What do you mean!?” Kasious and Glyka looked horrified.
“Well, the 6 months of funds to sustain me during my report are almost all spent on the free bread now. Why do you think I’ve been crashing in your shed instead of renting a room?”
“So then… very soon… it’s all over for us?” Kasious despaired, “It’s been a week; collection day is tomorrow…”
“Don’t worry… I’m out of money but not out of ideas. Everything is in place to get you an even better backer shortly.” Kat grinned.

Notes:
-	The early gag of this chapter about ancient Greeks not knowing what domesticated cats are comes from the fact that they really didn’t until around the 5th century BCE. This story takes place during the era of Hercules almost 1000 years before that, so ancient Greece would not have had many domesticated cats yet.
-	Even once the Greeks and Romans got cats from the Phoenicians they were grouped together with weasels, the main pest hunting pet. This fact blew my mind and there wasn’t a good place to put it in the full book Zeus Almighty so that’s why this side story exists.
-	Egypt being spelled as “Aegypt” is 1: to differentiate this fictional land inspired by ancient Egypt from the real world country of Egypt and 2: is actually accurate to how Egypt used to be spelled in ancient times.
-	The racism found even among the lowest in Graiac society is based on the Lyndon B. Johnson quote: “If you can convince the lowest white man he's better than the best colored man, he won't notice you're picking his pocket. Hell, give him somebody to look down on, and he'll empty his pockets for you.” 
-	People being mad about different bread types is a reference to the interesting phenomenon of people in dominant group getting upset when minority groups get even a crumb of acknowledgement or favor. For example, the classic complaint of people not having to “Dial 1 for English”, they want English to be the ONLY option. Workers at call centers have reported recurring examples of people complaining: "I shouldn't have to press any number for English, make the foreigners press the number."
-	In ancient Greek cities like Athens, girls got married around the age of 14 to men more than twice their age. Gross.
-	Kat Trivia: Nobody knows what specific cat breed Kat’s demi-human features are from. She jokingly always suggests she is a Schrodinger’s Cat.
