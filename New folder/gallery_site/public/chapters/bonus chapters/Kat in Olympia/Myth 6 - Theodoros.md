*Myth ?.514: Theódoros

PART 1
Theódoros was a miracle child. His parents wouldn’t stop telling him how they were unable to have a child until they were old and grey. Then fifteen years ago, the elderly couple was graced with him: surely a sign from the heavens. Hence his name Theódoros: gift from the gods.
Eupatridus was equally as protective of his son as he was his wife. His doting treatment equally violated Graia’s strict masculine norms for the man of the house. Yet the tyrant’s cruelty to outsiders was at the behest of having the freedom to be kind to his family and so he indulged.
Eupatridus gave his wife who grew up abused and world weary from a life of poverty a glorious and luxurious new lease on life. But Theódoros who had never known hardship became spoiled, only knowing the lap of luxury. His father’s men treated him like actual royalty; everyone did if they knew what was good for them. Theódoros knew how feared his father was and used it to get whatever he wanted. He enjoyed his good life.
That was why Theódoros was so taken aback by this girl showing him such utter disrespect! For the past 2 weeks she’d been running around causing trouble. He remembered how his father ranted and raved at her arrogant proclamations when they first met. And yet even though they were supposed to meet weekly, she kept annoying him with annoying questions whenever she had free time.
Some small bakery Theódoros mom had become obsessed with got a lucrative deal to give out fresh bread with Eupatridus’ name and likeness on it. Soon the whole town was hooked; Eupatridus was opening up new locations and putting former bakers to work under the direction of his own palace staff. Eupatridus caving on that one request from Kat through his wife seemed to have emboldened the girl to arrogantly demand the tyrant consider her other outlandish ideas.
However, the proud tyrant grew frustrated at her many suggestions and apparent thirst for influence. He shooed her away saying they would meet once a week and no more since he was a busy man.
“My son… be wary of her. That girl… it’s like she knows exactly what to say to get under my skin. Despite her friendly façade, she is surely trying to seize everything I care about as a means to control me. Just as she manipulates your mother against me, she may try to control you as well.” Eupatridus warned Theódoros.
Theódoros heeded his father’s advice but couldn’t help but laugh. Everything Eupatridus cared about was in danger? Why did Theódoros have to worry then? Since he’d become a teenager, his relationship with his father was defined by arguments and strife. The boy could not appreciate that if anyone other than he or his mother argued with Eupatridus they would be violently murdered.
He resented his father’s devotion to grooming him as a viable successor yet his distance and hesitance to show his son the truly messy world involved in tyranny. Eupatridus was stuck wanting to protect the boy he cherished while also wanting to create a man as ruthless as him who could keep his empire running. He couldn’t have both and his hesitant half measures were no match for Theódoros’ militant rejection of his father’s wishes.
He had no intention of seizing the family business. He kicked up fusses and sabotaged ceremonies and every attempt to integrate him into the Tyranny. Anything his father was for he was against; distrusting that annoying girl seemed to be all they held in common.

PART 2
The next day, Theódoros snuck out of his lessons only to be attacked!
“Hiya Theo! Can I call you Theo?” Kat seemed to appear out of nowhere.
“Where’d you come from!?” Theódoros jumped in surprise.
“Weaving with your mother while we had a quick chat.”
“Good for you.” Theódoros tried to walk away.
“It’s just that I’ve been here for a while, but we haven’t had a proper conversation yet. So, I was wondering if I could interview you for my report.”
“What are you talking about!?”
Theódoros turned around but Kat was gone. When he turned back around, she was in front of him with a smile.
“Leave me alone you creepy girl! We aren’t friends.”
“Not yet. But your mother wants us to be, she says you’re a bit of a loner.”
“Stop acting like you know me. You don’t.” Theódoros scoffed.
“Your mom’s told me a lot about you.” Kat smiled and took out her pen and tablet, “If you gave me an interview I could confirm or deny the allegations.”
“…what allegations?”
“That you have indeed become very self conscious.” Kat wrote down the confirmation.
“Go away! I have things to do.” Theódoros pouted and stormed off.
“Like what? You’re ditching your lessons.” Kat laughed.
Theódoros tensed up, “Did my mom tell you that too?”
“No, she told me you were at your lessons. Your tutor told me you were skipping them.”
“What’s it to you?”
“I know a little girl who would kill to have the education you’re taking for granted. Forgive me, but where I come from wastefulness is a serious vice.”
“If you’re that jealous of my lessons you can take them instead.”
“I wasn’t talking about myself before. Although now that you mention it, reviewing what people are learning in Olympia would be great for my report.”
“You’re so eager to write your report. Don’t tell me you actually like homework.”
“It isn’t homework; it is my duty as Reporter to the Assembly. In this role I’m actually much closer to the tutor than the student.”
“You? A tutor?”
“It’s more likely than you’d think. I have a proud history of tutoring my peers, juniors and even seniors in many matters.”
“That arrogance does actually remind me of my tutor.”
“Forgive that smug bragging. I would normally abstain, but I am not so subtly advertising my services.”
“What? You want to tutor me?”
“Yup yup.”
“Why?”
“Because the Academic Exam is next week and your mother is worried you won’t be prepared. Spiting your father isn’t worth ruining your education.”
Theódoros flinched.
“What? Going to try and guilt trip me into studying? It’s too late, isn’t it?”
“Nope. Why use guilt when there are other incentives.” Kat smiled before producing a scroll, “The other day, I watched the new Aristophanes play at Olympia’s festival to Dionysus. I was so intrigued that I recorded it all line by line for my report.”
Theódoros’ scowl softened the more Kat talked.
“It’s such a good play, many in the audience agreed it might be his best one yet. It didn’t win first place for nothing”
“First place?” he bit.
“Yeah, it beat out this year’s Atticus play; the sequel to last year’s first place. It was also very good by the way.” Kat said as she retrieved another scroll.
“Lucky you…” Theódoros looked away, using all his strength to avoid asking the tempting question.
“It’s a shame, your mom said that as much as you hate studying you love theatre. But with your father grounding you to study until the exam ends, you won’t be able to see that play until theatrics are put on again at next year’s festival to Dionysus.”
“Yes, I am very upset about that…” Theódoros played dumb.
“But… your mother and I agreed you could have these play transcripts if you pass next week’s exam.” Kat smiled.
Theódoros growled; his father was right this girl was devious! Strangely, she was serious about being his tutor, she personally oversaw his preparations for the exam. Worse, Picrosa invited Kat to stay in the palace as his official tutor until after the exam is over. 
The girl left her “guardian” pet with Kasious and Glyka to enter the house of Eupatridus. Now she had 24/7 access to bombard Theódoros with cramming for his exam.

PART 3
In Graia, education was for elites only and as such tutors focused on important skills for high society. Reading and writing for the power they unlocked, arithmetic for business, law and civics to mold ideal citizens, music for religious purposes, manners and etiquette for social capital and memorized epic poetry to fit into high-class common cause.
“Sign this contract of tutelage before we begin.” Kat smiled.
“Sure whatever.” Theódoros groaned as he signed.
“You really should’ve read that first.”
“What? Why?”
Kat just shrugged, leaving the document with him. What did he just agree to!? Later as Kat was watching construction on paved paths she’d requested, Theódoros rushed over in a panic.
“Why the hell do you want my liver!?” he panicked.
“Why’d you let me have it?” Kat asked.
“That isn’t funny! You can’t just do that!”
“Why not?”
Theódoros took note of her tone. It was a question she was awaiting a genuine response to.
“…because it is unethical?”
“Hah! That doesn’t stop most contracts. If you really want to know, check the footnote near the bottom left of the scroll.”
Theódoros did so and was shocked to find a provision that said the contract would come into effect after they both signed as well as Theódoros’ father.
“I knew that! I was the one testing you!” Theódoros cried.
“Always check contract footnotes and annotations. And always always always read them.” Kat wagged her finger.
“Whatever, we don’t need a contract…”
“Nah, it’s fine. Your mother and I already signed the real one. You’re paying by the way and my fee isn’t cheap. It’s about three months’ allowance.”
“Three months?”
“Unless of course you solve these.” Kat put forth a gigantic mathematics equation.
Theódoros spent hours trying to crack it before returning his answer.
“Wrong.” Kat immediately concluded.
“What!?”
“Some of these questions were on last year’s exam. With the answer key you did well, but I now see the weak points we can work on. Overall good job, take a break and enjoy this play transcript.”
Theódoros wasn’t complaining. It was one he’d already seen before, but it was a classic he enjoyed. When the break ended and he returned the scroll Kat smiled.
“How many crimes were committed in this story?”
“It’s a work of fiction; nobody pays attention to laws.”
“But if you, as a resident of Olympia, did this what would you get in trouble for doing?”
Kat quickly found ways to make questions around Theódoros’ various interests. After first testing him to gauge where he currently was she was ready to formally begin teaching him directly, attacking his weak points to ensure he wouldn’t be vulnerable on the exam.
“Why is the character old man Rom constantly calling on the goddess Hebe?”
“In this play they mentioned the first Cretan war is ongoing. Based on how it’s described, when do you think this was written?”
“This famous writer died penniless, which royalties’ contract would’ve given him the most financial security.”
Over the week, Theódoros hated to admit it, but she was kind of making learning fun. Instead of a general, mass consumable curriculum it targeted and encouraged his personal interests while also showing how he as an individual fit into a larger society. Strangely, the whole time she was taking notes too as if she was learning as well.
“Interesting. You’re only tested on writing in Linear A and Linear B. You don’t learn the alphabet at all here?” Kat asked after reviewing standard teaching materials.
“The alphabet? Aren’t those the strange Phoenician scribbles?” Theódoros raised an eyebrow.
“The Graiac adaptation of the alphabet is quite popular in other regions. Some cities even teach it as the main way to write. Some argue it should be the standard way to write.”
“That’s weird. We have our own writing systems, why do we need the Phoenicians’?”
“Funnily enough, some cities that adopted it refuse to acknowledge it’s foreign origin. Though it seems pretending Graia invented it isn’t universal in all cities.”
“Why would we pretend to invent the alphabet? It’s just some passing fad. I don’t think it’ll last.”
“We’ll see about that. Though you should know that an increasing number of plays are written in the alphabet.”
Theódoros was given pause, “Maybe we can review the alphabet later…”
“After we tighten up your Linear A penmanship. At times it can be utterly indecipherable.” Kat laughed as they went back to studying.
She had a nice way of splicing casual conversations with review. She often educated him on topics he wasn’t learning for the exam. After all, when would he need to know is ABCs? Such a nerd; she was just using this chance to cram his head full of any information she wanted.
“In this scene, what faux pas has our protagonist committed? Why are they laughing at him?” Kat asked.
“I don’t know; he’s a comic relief. Everyone laughs at him, the silly voice makes everything laughable.”
“The voice direction is to ensure everyone in the audience laughs along. However, there is a deeper joke that goes over even most fans’ heads.” Kat smiled.
She wasn’t wrong, it was a subtle obscure reference Theódoros never knew. However, the second it clicked for him he burst out laughing. This girl managed to find a way to deepen his love for his passion even more. He almost accidentally thanked her but stubbornly paused; why was she smiling at him like that. For some reason, her cute grin was irritating him.
“See, a lot of your favorite actors and playwrights were quite well educated. Sorry Theo, but you have to stay in school even if you’re going into show business.”
Theo smiled at first; of course, he didn’t have to tell her directly for the girl to realize it. However, his smile faded as he remembered something else.
“What does it matter. The old man wants me to be head of the family. He’d kill me if he knew I wanted to work in theatre. Even though he was the one who always used to bring me to see plays.” Theo sighed.
“As big and successful as theatre productions are, there isn’t actually a lot of money in it for writers or actors.”
“It’s not just about money! He doesn’t understand.”
“Your mom says you used to watch plays together as a family though.”
“Watching one and being a part of one are two different things. He’s always on about not working himself to death to elevate us just so I can sink down and work a popper’s position. You know how dads are.”
“I think our experiences are a bit different.”
“Oh right, you’re a girl. I guess everyone is expecting you to follow your mother instead.”
“Not really. My goal is to follow in my father’s footsteps, but it isn’t ‘cause he forced me or anything. All my older siblings took completely different paths, and he supported them all. Actually, me following him is the only career choice to get any pushback.”
“Career choice?” Theo was confused by what she meant.
“The point is, we have different circumstances. Your father doesn’t want you to work a job where you can’t make ends meet.”
“So, you’re taking his side?”
“Nope. I’m explaining his logic or at least a steel-man of it. He doesn’t want you being a thespian in a society that would punish you for it.”
“So, you want me to give up on it too?” Theo sighed. 
After the week of bonding, he’d gone and opened up to her about this. What was he thinking? And why was he so disappointed by the inevitable.
“Don’t give up on your dream Theo!” Kat rallied him, “All we have to do is change society and there will be no reason your dad can oppose it!”
Theo was given pause before he burst out laughing, “You really are ridiculous, you know that.”
“Lots of things are ridiculous in this world. Like the fee I’m charging you for tutoring. So, tell you what… the real exam is in 2 days. Pass this practice exam I’ve made for you and consider the fee waived.” Kat grinned, waving another scroll before him.
“You’re on.” Theo grinned.
The practice test was hard but after going all out, Theo was able to pass. Kat waived her tutor’s fee and declared his training was complete.
“What do we do now? I beat the practise, but the real deal is sure to be way harder! There’s only one day left; how can I prepare?” Theo had invested too much into the test to fail now.
“We chill. I have nothing left to teach you young grasshopper.” Kat gave a thumbs up.
Theo was baffled! They really did spend the last day just hanging out and recovering from the intense studying. Why was she taking it so easy with the big battle tomorrow? Other than occasional light reviews they didn’t do any serious studying on the final day! The only part they consistently went over was the epic poetry recital; Theo’s chance to flex his acting chops. What was she thinking?
Kat was so weird, but this strange girl was able to convince Theo that he had a chance… at the exam and his dream. 

PART 4
Then the day of the exam came, stressed and exhausted pupils filled the halls, surrounding the chilled out Theo. Theo was shocked; Kat had prepared him perfectly. Did she somehow know exactly what it would be about? Things he would’ve gotten wrong just a few days ago came easily to him. The boring details he always ignored now came easily to him via easy mnemonic devices or personal associations.
He completed the exam’s written portion and aced the epic poetry recital leaning on the acting practice he and Kat did during breaks. Theo came out of the exam with his head held high. The next day Theo and his parents went to see the results posted outside the exam hall. Many prestigious families from Olympia were there with mixed reactions of joy or outrage.
The house of Eupatridus was only met with confusion. Where was Theo’s name? Eupatridus was utterly panicking. Theo looked around confused. Then he saw Kat sitting in a tree far away, watching with a smile. She motioned away from where they looked to where he least expected.
Theo started heading where she motioned as his parent’s called after him. He was going the wrong way: that’s where the top 50 names were displayed with honors. They were shocked to find his name was in fact there. Theo could hardly believe it: he had ranked 47th out of the hundreds of elite families who took the test. 47th highest test score… in the largest city in Graia!
Theo was so shocked he couldn’t react as his honorary Petasos cap given to top 50 scorers was placed on his head.
“My son!!!” Eupatridus hugged him in public, overjoyed as he held him aloft with triumph.
Theo was shocked! When was the last time they were so close?
“We must celebrate!” Eupatridus decreed.
“Of course. We have already made preparations.” Pikrosa smiled having never doubted her son for a second.
She led them to the amphitheatre of Dionysus where plays were shown. It was weird, wasn’t the god of theatre’s festival over? That should’ve meant that the only way to see plays would be in smaller private venues. Yet Theo and various other theatre enthusiasts from both the slums and big city sat down as the world-renowned acting troupe performed.
When the show ended something strange happened. Instead of leaving the actors came out and started taking questions from the audience. In Graia nobody thought highly of actors. Who had questions to ask them.
A sea of hands were raised as the theatre kids having the time of their lives unleashed the questions for their idols. Eupatridus was stunned seeing the genuine joy on his son’s face. This experience seemed more exciting to him than placing in the top 50 on the exam! The room went silent when the Tyrant Eupatridus himself raised his hand for a question. Obviously, he was chosen to ask his next.
“Help me understand… what fills you with such passion for theatre…” he awkwardly asked.
Theo’s eyes widened. He actually wanted to know? Eupatridus still didn’t understand fully but seeing all this enthusiasm for a craft he looked down on let him get a bit closer to his son. On the way home they discussed the play and Eupatridus was even disappointed when Theo split from him to go talk to Kat. He was having fun? Talking with his son about his silly little indulgence?
“Hiya Theo.” Kat waved.
“Where were you this whole time!?” he cried.
“I wanted to get a behind the scenes view of theatre productions for my report. I was watching from behind the scenes. Congrats on the exam by the way.”
“Oh yeah! How- did you cheat or fix the results?”
“Nah, we didn’t need to you’re smarter than you think.”
“What? Stop trying to suck up to me, I’m not paying out!” Theo stammered uncontrollably unable to deal with direct compliments.
“Also, I’ve been reading a lot of critiques on Graia’s standardized exams and how easy they are to meta-game! Turns out all those books I read were right on the money!” Kat beamed, “Thanks for helping me with the experiment!”
Just as Eupatridus was confused by Theo’s passion he was baffled by her strange enthusiasms. His love of fiction seemed to mirror her obsession with nonfiction “nerdy” texts. Yet as weird as it was, he found it adorable.
“You’re so weird. You helped my mom arrange the play today?” Theo asked, “Even a tyrant would have trouble booking a private venue this big with such prestigious troupe.”
“Yep. They were against the idea at first but turns out I could bribe them in much the same way I did with you. Turns out I had a copy of some plays they were dying to get copies of.” Kat smiled.
“…how? Why do you know so much about theatre anyways?”
“Didn’t I mention? My big brother is a playwright. He learned from my uncle who’s a pretty big deal too. Even mentioning I had some of their compositions sealed the deal.”
“Really!? Who are they, I might know them!” 
“I doubt it, I don’t think my brother’s plays are shown in Graia.”
Theo sighed, she was still trying to play the strange, mysterious girl; how annoying. Yet, Theo was very disappointed to see her leave now that her tutoring duties were done.
“I heard my mom wants you to stay…” he awkwardly insisted.
“Really? Because she already said her farewells.” Kat tilted her head.
“Yeah well… sometimes she can be proud and hide how she really feels to save face.”
“That doesn't sound like her.”
“The point is… that guest room isn’t going anywhere.”
“Maybe I can visit from time to time but Glyka keeps mentioning how much she misses me. I can’t make a little kid cry, now can I?”
“Fine… get lost then.” Theo trembled and stormed off.
However, as Kat was walking away he ran back and called after her, “Thank you! I’ll see you around!”

-	Theódoros originally had a different name. I just can’t remember what it is. When looking through my old story notes from early 2024 I found constant mentions of his story but only calling him “Eupatridus’ son” I have no idea what his name was: the file with more details is lost now.
-	Real Historical Education in ancient Greece: 
-	How plays worked in Greece is very different from what is presented in this story. Plays were only performed once at festivals and then never again
-	Transcripts of plays weren’t really a thing because the Greeks believed that writing something down stifled its ability to change and grow, preferring oral story telling. In this sitting the adaptation is that transcripts are incredibly rare and usually illegally produced.
-	On Theo’s questions: 1) old man Rom is a Re;Zero reference. He calls on Hebe the goddess of youth to save him from old age. 2) The First Cretan war was around 70 years before this story takes place
-	The Greek alphabet was derived from the Phoenician writing system. Before developing the alphabet the Greeks used Linear A (1800–1450 BC) and Linear B (1450–1200 BC).
-	In this fictional adaptation, the story takes place when Linear B has just been introduced as a simplified modern version of Linear A similar to simplified chinese vs traditional Chinese or printing vs cursive. In the story, Linear B is being promoted by xenophobic cities such as Olympia who know that Linear A needs to be replaced but are too racist to take inspiration from the foreign Phoencians
-	In real life the Greek alphabet was developed around 800 BC, long after this Bronze age story would have taken place. The idea is that in this fantasy setting some more open minded cities (like Kat’s hometown) accelerated the alphabet’s development because they were more open to learning from other cultures than real world ancient Greeks. More on the history of the alphabet: History_of_the_alphabet 
-	The joke about Theo’s Linear A writing being undecipherable is a joke referencing the fact that while Linear B has been deciphered, nobody knows how to read Linear A even today.

Obviously my editors would never allow such a wordcount devourer to exist so I still never have but this chapter was a best approach of that.
