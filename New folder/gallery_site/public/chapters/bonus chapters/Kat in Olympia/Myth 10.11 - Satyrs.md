*Myth ?.518: Satyrs

In many ways demi-humans were seen as foreigners: savage beasts that no place in human cities like Olympia. And yet some demi-humans, satyrs specifically, had a longer history with Olympia and its eponymous mountain than any humans did.
Long before there were any human or even divine settlements near Mount Olympus it was originally inhabited by nomadic satyr people. Satyrs were demi-humans who were humans on top with a mountain goat’s lower body and horns.
The indigenous satyrs called the region Olympus, but the mountain was Mount Mytikas (the nose Mountain) after an epithet of one of their pre-Olympian gods. When Ouranos the original god of Graia created his “good Empire” he made contact with them and invited them to join under his protection. As their protector he promised to keep out the greedy human merchants who learned of the region’s rich deposits of Adamant and so wanted to plunder it. Under Ouranos the native territory was honored.
After the evil Titans overthrew Ouranos, they made their base of operations in Tartarus Tower: a giant futuristic fortress of black Adamant built on top of Mount Othrys. Their secondary foothold however was on Mount Olympus, their main mine which powered the imperial machine. Unlike Ouranos, the Titan Empire did not care about the satyrs’ native rights to the land, those who resisted were killed or enslaved and made to take part in the mining of their homeland.
It was this destruction of his peoples’ homeland that made the satyr god Pan rebel against the Titans and join the Olympian revolution. Mount Olympus was the Olympian revolution’s staging ground for the final Battle with the Titans. 
The Olympian rebels struck there first to starve out the Titan capital and cut off their Adamant supply. From there the final siege began, their home base Olympus stayed floating above the ruins of the mountain which the Titans destroyed rather than let the rebels take it. After Zeus and the Olympians drove the Titans out of their main mine, they laid waste to Tartarus Tower and Mount Othrys, winning the Titan War.
Pan’s victorious shout became a symbol of victory for the Olympians. The Satyrs at first rejoiced and thanked Zeus however it quickly became clear that he hadn’t done it for them. Zeus declared that Olympus was his sacred mountain and would serve as the heart of his new domain. The Satyrs were expelled and forced to live in the town that popped up at the base of the mountain.
While the Titans succeeded in destroying the main mountain itself, their hasty withdrawal and demolition had left rich Adamant deposits in surrounding peaks and underground parts of the mine. Zeus was quick to re-open these mining operations to fill the Olympian treasury. After all, Zeus had declared that everything the Titans once owned that was not claimed by an ally was his to keep.
The satyrs’ protests were ignored; nothing had changed. They appealed to the most prominent satyr ally of Zeus: Pan for help. Pan however refused to cross his new boss. Instead, Pan bowed to Zeus and allowed the takeover of his home but instead offered the satyrs a place to live with him in the woodlands Zeus gave him as a reward for his loyalty.
The Olympian Satyr community was still torn down the middle between those who respect Pan and the satyrs who followed him to become “woodland satyrs” because this was the best they could hope for in the face of Zeus. Opposing them were those who reject this capitulation to colonizers and still demanded their mountain back from Graia who is violating the treaties with Ouranos. To them big mountain which the humans stole was not Mount Olympus, it was still Mount Mytikas.
“Thank you for the history lesson, father but we already knew that.” Pan, Olympian god of the woodlands rolled his eyes.
“Father?” Aegipan, patriarch of the mountain satyrs scoffed, “Penelope. Doesn’t this traitor call himself the son of Hermes now.” The man growled.
“Do not be difficult my love.” Penelope, wife of Aegipan sighed.
“Do not waste your sympathies on a sandal-licking race traitor.” Aegipan pointed at Pan.
“Political differences aside, he is our son. I still gave birth to him.” Penelope tried to calm her fuming husband.
“Did you? Because he has been going around telling people he was born of some unnamed daughter of Dryops. Or perhaps another nymph named Oenis. Or a goat with an overly friendly husbandman.” Aegipan’s rage on his wife’s behalf was palatable.
His mother never confronted him on it, but Pan still avoided eye contact in shame.
“For Zeus’ token satyr you sure are ashamed to admit any actual satyr heritage. One of these days you might remember no matter how much you sell out to them you will never be a human.” Aegipan snarled.
“I knew coming here was a mistake.” Pan began to stand up, but his mother motioned for him to stop.
“It was long overdo for us to be reunited. Let us not cut it short. Instead, let us thank the kind young lady who made it happen.” Penelope smiled at Kat who was sitting silently next to Pan.
“I’ve recently been interviewing people from Pan’s woodland reservation and learned of your people’s predicament. I was hoping to get your side of the story… and help ease tensions if I could.” Kat smiled.
“Ease tension with this brat’s league of sellouts?” Aegipan rolled his eyes.
“Complain all you want old man, but woodland satyrs are living respectable new lives while you old fashioned fools dwindle to nothing!”
“Yes, I’ve noticed you seducing our youth away to be Zeus’ pets.”
“Pets!? The woodlands aren’t perfect, but it is an infinitely better future for our people than this slums you preside over!”
Back and forth the father and son argued, tensions flaring up at the slightest touched nerve.
“Such an utter lack of respect- did you pick that up from how the humans talk about us?” Aegipan snarled.
“Stop acting high and mighty old man; nobody respects you.” Pan pointed.
“You’ve ensured that. Your satyr jesters sell us out; acting like fools and playing into human bigotries to earn a quick buck. You sing and dance for them as a caricature as lustful, dimwitted drunkards!”
Pan tensed up. That stereotype was a sore spot for him too. He fought alongside Zeus himself but now was shown no respect; he was seen as a sidekick or a pet, not an equal. Were his compromises really contributing to that.
“You’re an embarrassment. And nobody respects you either.” Aegipan harshly reiterated his son’s own insecurities.
That rude tone was enough o shatter Pan’s self reflection and launch him back into emotional bickering. These extremely sensitive topics were bringing out the worst in both men and yet, Penelope the peacemaker calmed them both. She was at once able to steer the conversation back to civility while also steel manning and respecting both interlocutors. Where Aegipan and Pan’s wittiness could be muted by strong emotion, Penelope was always on point. Kat smiled, taking note of Penelope’s rumored negotiating skills.
“Now then young lady, are you sure there aren’t any additional things you wanted by visiting us? I’ve heard you’ve been making some interesting moves in the human slums as of late.” Penelope turned her attention to Kat.
“Ah, you must have spoken to some of Mount Mytikas pilgrims I ran into on their travels.” Kat smiled.
“It is an important, difficult and expensive rite of passage for our people. I appreciate the help you showed them when they stayed in your midst.” Penelope replied.
“I’m happy to help, I just wish I could’ve done more. The satyr community seems to have many unaddressed problems.”
“They are our community’s problems… unless you are ready to admit your intent to involve yourselves in satyr affairs.”
The two boys were mostly at a loss. Each was so focused on their reunion with the other that they hadn’t paid Kat’s presence or Penelope’s research and preparations much mind. However, as the father and son bickered, they missed the two women’s silent maneuvering.
“Young lady… your humility betrays you. The clout required to gain an audience with the god Pan confirms our suspicion on who has sent you. You clearly have plans for satyrs, we just hope you aren’t mixing us up in anything unsavory.”
“I wouldn’t dream of it.” Kat smiled, but she saw Aegipan had calmed and was now fully engaged.
His wife had effectively re-activated the shrewd schemer with whom she shared power. Of one mind, the shrewd couple inspected Kat more closely.
“Your projects in the slums require capital and resources. We are wanting in one but not the other. So, tell me, are you here about the mineral deposits those damned human speculators found on our land?” Aegipan asked.
“Of course not. I know that satyrs have sworn off occupations like mining after the era of servitude under the Titans. I would never open old wounds for your people.” Kat assured him.
“Even if you don’t want us for mining slaves, humans could extract it from us just as they’d done before.”
“I have no interest in your land’s resources. It is human resources, I assure you.”
“So, it is slaves then.” Aegipan rolled his eyes.
“You won’t make us mine but knowing the dire conditions of our people there is other work you could force on desperate satyrs.” Penelope noted.
“I understand that past dealings with outsiders has filled you with skepticism, but the jobs I want to offer are good paying jobs. I am not looking to expropriate or pull from you, but I want truly equal exchange. An alliance between your people and mine so we can exchange goods and services as equals.” Kat revealed.
Pan was shocked. She hadn’t mentioned any of that to him. She’d grown so crafty and ambitious since they last met.
“You want us to ally and trade with you? Why so you can abuse and steal from us as the humans do? Why should we trust your offer to trade?” Aegipan noted.
“Because you would be in charge of our people’s relations… on both sides.” Kat smiled making the three satyrs pause in disbelief.
“You, are the patriarch of the satyrs and I trust you will represent your people well. Especially since on our side I would be honored to have the wise and crafty Penelope as our representative to the satyrs in charge of our affairs. I hope it won’t be too difficult for you two to work together. I’m sure your marital homophrsene will lead to smooth relations between our people.” Kat explained to their shock.
She was essentially giving the satyrs unchecked power in this relationship. It was almost too good to be true.
“What do you get out of it?” crafty Penelope asked.
“Your presence alone is a great help. Having the mother of a god on our side will add great legitimacy to our cause and also be a pillar of support for the demihuman people who right now are oppressed and without many good icons to aspire to.”
“Really? To what end?”
“By pooling our resources and efforts, I plan to allow satyrs of the city as well as the woodland and mountain reservations free and easy access to Mount Mytikas for their pilgrimage. We will allow integration and greater access to the land of their ancestors. By working together, it can be done. Three communities working as one for the benefit of satyrs everywhere.” Kat declared.
“It sounds almost too good to be true. You are offering us paradise.” Penelope restrained her hope with reason.
Kat did not hesitate and replied, “It won’t be easy, but if we all work together, we shall attain paradise. A heaven in the world; the peak of Mytikas on the ground. I ask you all join me in this endeavor.”

-	Oxford dictionary describes satyrs as: “one of a class of lustful, drunken woodland gods. In Greek art they were represented as a man with a horse's ears and tail, but in Roman representations as a man with a goat's ears, tail, legs, and horns.”
-	Aegipan literally means “goat pan” and might just be one of Pan’s alternate names that was mistakenly described as his father in later myths. In this adaptation basically all myths related to both Aegipan and Pan are about Pan himself. His father bears Aegipan’s name but is a wholly new character
-	In mythology, Pan’s mother is very mysterious and strange with wildly different parentage. Some say his mother was some unnamed daughter of Dryops, while others say it was Penelope (possibly the famous wife of Odysseus). Quoting one source: “According to Hard, the idea of Penelope being the mother "is so odd that it is tempting to suppose that this Penelope was not originally the wife of Odysseus, but an entirely different figure, perhaps an Arcadian nymph or the above-mentioned daughter of Dryops"”

-	The two types of satyrs being woodland and mountain satyrs is a reference to the differences between mountain goats and regular goats
