*Myth 15.65: Conclusion

Working together, Mel and Hydra laid waste to the villains that had usurped her father’s throne. After 30 years, an entire generation in captivity the colony of Minoa was freed as Crete once again. As Melissa carried the hobbling Hydra over her shoulder, the two were greeted by a celebratory and adoring crowd.
“Hail Melissa, Queen of Crete! Our liberator and savior!” the great crowd cheered for the returning princess in one earnest voice.
“Queen of Crete?” Melissa asked in bafflement.
“Of course, daughter of Melisseus. It only makes sense.” An elder of the peasants stepped forward and bowed.
Mel and Hydra looked at each other in confusion. This wasn’t part of the plan.
“There are many of us who remember the virtuous rule of your father. We are ashamed that we ever fell for Minos’ lies and betrayed him. We old fools beg for your forgiveness, that our children and our children’s children may again prosper under the rule of a Melisseus.” The elder bowed more deeply, inspiring others of his generation to do the same.
“You would have me make myself a tyrant? So soon after escaping the shadow of another?” Melissa demanded.
“This is different. The throne is your birthright. We saw how you fought for us; you would rule wisely and justly just like your father.” The elders pushed as the nostalgically remembered the previous monarchy.
Mel prepared to argue against them. After seeing the world and learning so much… after letting that idiot Hydra chat her ear off about political philosophy, she knew that was wrong. She had to correct this antiquated notion. However, before she could another elder spoke up.
“You and your husband can continue, the Melisseus bloodline so that Crete may be ruled by Cretans again.”
“He’s not my husband!” Mel rushed to correct the record, dropping Hydra in a flustered panic.
“Ow…” Hydra groaned from the floor.
“Sorry…” she bent down and helped him stand back up, “He’s not my husband. He’s just an… ally of mine.” She repeated more calmly.
“Ouch. Not even friend?” Hydra laughed despite his pain.
“Quiet, you.” Mel grumbled.
“Nonetheless, you are our queen! We need no king when we have you to lead us!” another elder insisted.
Mel saw their genuine pleas and pause. However, she looked to the youth: those who had only known kings as cruel and exploitative. They didn’t seem so eager to prostrate themselves before another, tradition be damned. Mel smiled.
“Lead yourselves.” Melissa declared to the elders’ shock.
“You’re right, I will fight for you. I am stronger now than my father ever was! But it wasn’t his strength that made him a good leader. That quality of just rule and competent administration is something I know I don’t have. I’ve never wanted to be queen. I’m a warrior, not a ruler.” Melissa explained.
“Then… who should lead?” the confused elders asked.
“Weren’t you listening? Weren’t you paying attention? I said lead yourselves. My father did the best he could as king; he tried to make the best monarchy possible. But he always wished for an alternative. He wished for a form of government headed by representatives and not rulers. We can achieve his goal. Rather than rely on a king that ruled on behalf of the people, Crete can become a place where the people ruled themselves just like you had shown yourselves to be capable of in the trial.” Melissa insisted to the crowd’s surprise.
A great murmur arose from them as Hydra smiled proudly.
“Just as you cannot rely on me to rule well just because my father did, power cannot simply be passed by birthright. You saw how it went under the Minos dynasty: increasingly incompetent fools, each crueler than the last. Instead, elect leaders that align with popular will. Pick people who work for you and not the other way around. Not a government of monarchy or tyranny, but one by the people and for the people! That is Crete’s future!” Mel rallied her people.
Overnight, Crete became a democracy. It might have been a small step, but now Mel and Hydra were on track to bring about their dream. Mel agreed with Hades’ democratic goals but doubted his peaceful methodology. Her way seemed to work much better.
The other evil nobles and their cronies were imprisoned after the battle was won. An exhausted Hydra congratulated Mel on the win. Though he was so tired he couldn’t even stand up or shake her hand.
“This is the last time I come to an assassination in person.” Hydra groaned in pain as he laid motionless on the ground.
“You really exhausted yourself, huh?” Mel asked.
“Well, it was that or die.” Hydra noted.
“Fair enough. Get rested up, you’re still scheduled to kill Procrustes tomorrow.” Mel added.
“Aw…do I have to. I can barely move.” Hydra whined.
“We just knocked over one of Zeus’ highest-ranking cronies. If we still want the element of surprise, we have to move quickly before he knows we’re coming.” Mel noted.
“Yeah… guess I’ll need to heal up quick then.” Hydra laughed weakly.
Mel paused. She hadn’t really thought about it, but this selfish little side quest did really put Hydra’s plan in jeopardy. It was just her quest so then why…
“Hydra… why did you come with me? This was my battle and I just had tunnel vision and… I could’ve ruined your entire revolution. Why?” Mel demanded.
“You wanted to kill Minos and save your home. It meant a lot to you, so I had to help. If it means a lot to you, it means a lot to me.” Hydra explained.
“What!?” Mel turned beet red at the sincerity he said that with. She practically squeaked her surprised question out.
“What’s wrong? I’m tired to turn my head. Why do you sound like that?” the immobile Hydra asked.
“Shut up! Why not just let me go on my own! I could’ve handled it.” Mel insisted.
“Mel. Come on. We both know you would’ve died if you ran that gauntlet alone.” Hydra pointed out.
“I said shut up! If you weren’t so weak right now, I’d smash you with my hammer!” an exasperated Mel cried.
“If you’re going to crush me at least leave my face intact. I want to leave a pretty corpse.” Hydra laughed until it hurt.
“You’re an idiot. I can’t believe I signed up to work with you.” Mel sighed. But though Hydra couldn’t see it, she was smiling.
