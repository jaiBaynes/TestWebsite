*Myth 15.5: King Minos of Crete

A great funeral was held for the Late King Minos the First.
However, when it turned out that few people agreed to attend, the event was changed to a family-only affair. To spare embarrassment they made it seem like they wanted few special mourners. It definitely wasn’t that Minos was so reviled that none would come to mourn him.
A grand feast was held on Crete to celebrate their triumphant conquest by the Great Minos. The Great King Minos in his old age had retired and passed his throne to his three sons: Minos Junior, Rhadamanthus and Sarpedon. The three sons were the ones hosting this party for all of Crete’s elite in honor of their great father.
After all, it had been twenty-five glorious years since Minos Senior’s conquest of Crete for Graia. Serving the great god Zeus, this colonization effort had yielded great fortune for Olympus. It was this fortune the three co-monarchs were sure to put on display: each trying to outdo the other with their extravagance and superfluity.
It was also this extracted wealth that moved the starving native Cretans outside as they wailed at their misfortune. They watched all the splendor, and plenty be siphoned from them into the palace for the kings and their cronies. But their pleas fell on deaf ears. Melisseus, the last native king of Crete before the colonization was only a distant memory.
Now that it was far too late, they realized that hade been duped and that they were better off under Melisseus. Being Minoans under Minos paled in comparison to the glory days of being Melisseus’ Cretans.
Melisseus was a “good king” because he listened to his people, but that was a courtesy. The kingship in anyone else’s hands cared not for public opinion. When the “good king” Melisseus was gone, the next king could not be relied on to rule in the same way. Minos was even worse than that: he resented public opinion and made a show that if the people wanted anything and pestered him with such whining, he would do the opposite. His sons learned well from him.
After all, a king’s judgement alone was what was needed to rule. They were rulers, not representatives: the people worked for the king and not the other way around. That was the way of Minos.
Speaking of which, the former King Minos was running rather late. This party had been planned months in advance of his sudden and mysterious disappearance. With his kingship behind him, the king went about tying up loose ends and hunting down the escaped engineer Daedalus. It was a long story, one for another time.
Minos Junior, who hosted the party at his Palace of Knossos could wait no longer. He had gone to glance off his balcony to see if his father would return, however instead he was greeted by the starving and angered peasantry, begging for anything he could spare.
“Cretins.” Minos said while glaring down at his own starving populace.
Turning away and returning to his party, he began to entertain guests, informing them that unfortunately their father was a no show. Either way, he would want them to enjoy themselves in his absence.
“We’ll try as best we can Minos. But so close to windows, we can’t help but hear that rabble you’ve failed to keep under control.” Sarpedon, the jealous brother quipped. Minos growled. The other guests were whispering. What kind of ruler couldn’t quiet down the peasantry when entertaining such high-status guests.
“Ladies and gentlemen: esteemed guests of Minoa we stand here today in a palace temple that once served as the home of lord Zeus himself. Its divine accommodations should be apparent, but for you all tonight I would like to present some upgrades I installed myself.” Minos changed the subject as he sat on his throne and began to fiddle with the controls on the arm rest.
As he did, the walls of the dining hall began to display various vistas.
“Behold, a live view around gorgeous Minoa. These visuals are being taken in live from outside. We can dine on the sandy beach, or atop a mountain or even on the surface of the water off this beautiful island.” Minos explained while shifting between various views.
The pre-bronze age humans were mystified by this magic piece of technology. Minos was pleased. He’d paid a pretty penny for this divine technology. As rich as they were, even kings rarely got to see the otherworldly advancements available only to the gods. Given the awed reaction it was money well spent. It was worth what a hundred peasants would make in a thousand years, so it better have been worth it.
However, as Minos kept flipping through all the different angles, he eventually came to a view of the Palace of Knossos. The palace looked as beautiful as always, but it was still surrounded by that accursed mob. The gasps of awe turned to snickering and whispers.
“Oh dear, brother I don’t think you meant to show us that. From that high angle, the mob is even larger than we thought. I’d die of embarrassment if even a fraction of my subjects were acting like that under my watch.” Rhadamanthus laughed at his brother.
“Of course, there’d be that many if we can hear them from up here. The evening air is delightful, but perhaps we should relocate to a room farther inside to keep the racket out.” Sarpedon chimed in.
If two of the three brothers could ever agree on anything, it usually involved hassling the third. Minos tightened his fist and turned to his head guard.
“Go shut them up. Make that unsightly mob disappear. Scare them up so they know never to do this again. So, say I, King Minos of Minoa!” Minos made a spectacle of declaring.
The troops marched off to do as they were told. But at the same time, a disgusted servant made his exit from the room as well. Without anyone noticing, he snuck away to a place not protected by the palace’s Adamant shielding. After all, just as was planned he served as the destination point for Hydra and Mel’s teleportation.
Hydra, the son of Typhon and Melissa the deposed Princess of Crete appeared before the man, to his amazement. The inside man welcomed the two elite assassins to Crete. Their goal: assassinate King Minos and his brothers!
