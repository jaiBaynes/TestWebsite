*Myth 2: Hercules vs Apollo
 
Artwork: Hercules vs Apollo (placeholder)

Delphi lay in ruins, but the battle between Hercules and Apollo raged on. Apollo was a god; he should’ve been far stronger than a mere man. So, why wouldn’t he just die? The inferno of the sun itself failed to kill him. No, Apollo could sense the intensity of Hercules’ own supernatural Power whenever he shielded or counterattacked. Apollo’s blasts should have been more than enough to burn him to a crisp.
As the explosion faded again, Hercules remained unharmed. Yet, swift Apollo noticed that for just a second, he saw sparks surrounding Hercules, protecting Hercules. As the glare of the explosion died down, the god of light swore that just for an instant, he saw bolts of lightning retreat from around Hercules back into his club.
Was that aura of lightning what repelled all supernatural damage? Then why did the oaf raise his own pitiful shield as if was his own Power blocking the explosion? Did he not know?
“Hey, Apollo, stop being such a woman. Instead of running away and taking potshots like a coward why don’t you come up close and fight me like a man?” Hercules motioned for Apollo to come closer.
“Alas, your wits have made a departure.” Apollo shook his head, “You forget I am the divine archer.”
Apollo drew back his bowstring yet again, but this time he loaded a regular looking arrow rather than a sunshot. Hercules braced himself, ready to counterattack the instant the attack bounced off his mighty muscles. The arrow flew and hit him in the arm which held his club. Hercules was surprised; it drew blood, but he quickly pried the arrow out.
“As pathetic as the other shots were, you probably should’ve stuck with those.” Hercules pointed his club at Apollo.
It took him a second to realize that his hand was sickening and rotting at an alarming rate! The infection was spreading, eating him alive until Hercules disarmed himself both metaphorically and literally.
“I can give and take away health. Of plagues I command a great wealth.” Apollo chuckled.
So, it seemed that whatever strange protection that weapon granted, it only guarded against purely supernatural attacks rather than physical ones. That made sense, while Hercules excelled in using Power for physical enhancement, his club protected him against opponents like Apollo who dominated him in the nonphysical applications of Power. But this supernatural spell was contained in a physical delivery; it seemed the club could do nothing.
 Hercules desperately tried to seize his fallen weapon with his remaining hand but had to break away and dodge Apollo’s hail of plague arrows! The arrows whisked past the greatest hero and so, he could breathe a sigh of relief. Or so he thought. Hercules turned and found that the arrows that had missed him turned back around and were again in hot pursuit! If even one touched somewhere on his body, he couldn’t remove… he was dead.
Then, inspiration struck the hero. Apollo was very confused to see that instead of running away, Hercules removed the lion skin draped over his shoulders and used it as a shield. Unbelievably, the arrows bounced off the golden fur.
Apollo’s stunned reaction kept him from reacting in time as Hercules leapt to retrieve his fallen club. As soon as he touched the bronze weapon, a bolt fell from the heavens and struck him. Did he call those bolts down himself? Why did that lightning feel like…
The god of Delphi’s ponderings were interrupted when he realized Hercules’ arm was fully restored. That was impossible; he was a mere mortal. How was able to regenerate his arm as if he had a god’s body?
Apollo’s stunned pondering left him wide open. As quick as lightning, Hercules sent him flying with the swing of his club. Apollo was in disbelief; the mortal hero was now faster than the god of light? What in the Kosmos was going on?
Elsewhere in Delphi, many of the survivors gathered around Apollo’s temple. It was one of the few prized structures Apollo had taken great care to spare from the destruction. The recovering citizens grew worried about the distant thunder and shockwaves. Ironically, many prayed to Apollo for protection only for Apollo to come crashing into his temple!
Hercules landed on top of him; his foot planted on Apollo’s chest. All Apollo’s worshippers recoiled in horror at the sight of a mere human triumphing over the subject of their worship.
“It’s over, puny god! Before all your followers, tell them the truth! Tell them who is the greatest son of Zeus!” Hercules demanded.
Apollo glowed like the sun and blasted Hercules back. “Silence, hubristic fraud! Now, suffer the wrath of a god!”
In a flash of light, he dragged Hercules into his divine Microcosm: Supernova. Gods were revered as invincible not only because of their raw Power. The Titans, Olympus’s ancient enemies, were even stronger than the gods in terms of raw strength. To triumph against them, they needed something else. Microcosms were the ultimate manifestation of Power: divinely inspired domains outside the natural world and its limits. With their strength at its peak, there was nowhere more dangerous to fight a god than in their domain.
In Apollo’s Microcosm: Supernova, it was just Hercules, Apollo, and a massive, supergiant star. A brilliant detonation erupted faster than Hercules could perceive. The temperature of the Microcosm instantly hit the universal heat limit: 142 nonillion kelvins. Such a stellar explosion would’ve been impossible in nature, but this was Apollo’s Supernature. 
Hercules’s skin was instantly vaporized, and his Power barrier blasted apart. The hero could hardly even comprehend all the pain of having Apollo’s wrath detonated in his face. That was the power of a god.
Finally, the Microcosm ended, returning Apollo and Hercules to the real world. Apollo was unphased, immune to the wrath of his own domain. Meanwhile, Hercules was a charred, mostly skeletal husk on the ground. The sun god was impressed that the Nemean Lion’s skin was still intact after his ultimate attack. Apollo reached down to pull it off Hercules’s corpse, only to be interrupted by his raspy breathing. The god sighed before creating a miniature sun to blow him up. He’d pull the trophy pelt from his ashes. But then the sun god had a better idea.
“For being such a nuisance to me; I shall render you a laurel tree. You will redeem the favor of this god…once you’ve been carved into my new tripod…” Apollo cackled.
This was no idle threat: Olympian gods could use their Power to change the shape of anything they willed. When his Power protected him, Hercules was immune, but in such a weakened state, he was just as vulnerable as any other mortal. Any who angered the good gods of Graia were a snap away from inanimacy.
But Apollo had tarried too long. Once again, a bolt of divine lightning fell from the heavens and struck Hercules. In an instant, he was restored to full strength, and his body was fully healed.
“How!?” Apollo panicked at this deus ex machina.
“Because!” Hercules grabbed Apollo’s arm, “I am the son of Zeus!!!”
Apollo, god of prophecy, froze in utter shock at this outcome. Ironically, he did not see this coming. Now the only thing he was saying was “Ow.”
Hercules let loose with his club, battering and smashing Apollo with his full strength. The discombobulated and fatigued Apollo was helpless. Each hit caused him to lose more and more Power until Apollo was nearly exhausted. 
But the violence didn’t stop then. The berserk Hercules kept smashing Apollo over and over. With each slam, the town shook. Onlookers witnessed the horror of the beautiful god’s utter dismemberment: his attractive form twisted and disfigured in every way a man might fear.
Some wanted to pray; but to who? The worshippers’ only respite was the comfort of Apollo’s immortality: no matter what, the undying god could not be killed. However, this ironically was yet another myth of divinity.
Sure, the gods would not die of old age, and they could regenerate nearly any damage…but if their brains or vital organs were damaged too severely, that was that. In truth, Apollo was fighting for his life; even the “undying” feared he might die. He’d lost the actual fight, but now all his remaining Power was desperately poured into regeneration and energy shields to preserve his head and torso. Everything else had been mashed to paste by Hercules’s heroic hits.
Then, after shattering his pathetic barrier for the hundredth time, Hercules noticed that Apollo could raise no others. His wounds stopped healing and the god desperately hyperventilated: now needing air to survive like a human. Hercules smiled; Apollo was out of Power, and he was but a mortal man now. 
The puny god’s labored breathing was interrupted by a violent stomp on his chest.
“That’s what you get…for crossing the son of Zeus., Hercules said as he prepared to finish his opponent. 
The club was swung, but in a flash of lightning, Zeus appeared.
“That’s enough, my son. With you, I am well pleased,” Zeus stopped him.
Those words hurt Apollo more than any physical damage Hercules had inflicted. 
Speaking of damage, as Hercules looked up, he saw the devastation he’d unleashed on Delphi. His feeling of triumph, even if briefly, wavered. However, such thoughts were quickly put out of his mind by the people of Delphi, whose homes had just been destroyed, coming to worship and praise the menaces responsible.
Generations of living under Zeus’s thumb had left them like this, licking the sandals of the very gods who brought disaster upon them. They were honored that the gods graced the rubble of their homes with their presence. Zeus and Hercules smiled.
“The battle is won, father…but the Oracles did not reveal my next Labor to me,” Hercules informed his father. 
“Ah, you misunderstood. I told you that you shall receive your Final Labor on Delphi, but I never said it was from Apollo’s seers.” Zeus laughed and took Hercules under his arm.
“No, all this was Fated to happen. Apollo was but a stepping stone for you; a final test prepared to ensure you were ready to receive your marching orders from me directly!” Zeus declared. “My son, you have proven yourself worthy of the ultimate opponent! For your 12th and final labor, you shall descend into the Underworld and defeat the bane of the gods: the mightiest of the Monster-King Typhon’s spawn! Hercules, you shall earn eternal glory and fame by defeating Hades’s demon guard dog: Cerberus!”
