*Myth 1: Zeus and Lycaon - Wolf in Sheep’s Clothing

Dike, the goddess of human justice and servant of the king of the gods scoured the heavens for her master. As per usual “Zeus the loud thunderer that saw far and wide” was shirking his divine duties and had wandered off to find some humans he could amuse himself with. The issue was, Dike, who often had to pick up much of the slack needed him back on Olympus for some red tape classified approval or the other that she wasn’t allowed to do on her own.
She finally found him in the skies of Arcadia, looking down on a group of humans that had gathered atop mount Lykaon. What was he doing there?
About a month prior, king Lykaon went up to the mountain that shared his name alone. As the young, new king of the Arcadians he had gone through much effort to prepare himself for setting foot on the holy site. He removed his sandals as he set foot on the mountain sacred to his people and prepared.
After all, his people the Arcadians were devout followers of the cult of Zeus and in their hour of need, the king was going to pray to him on their behalf. War was coming with their neighbours since resources were scarce during these hard times in the world of men. As the two sides desperately fought over whatever scraps were left, they needed any edge they could get.
Thus, this was not just any old prayer where people would call out to Zeus alone, this was the real deal: a cult Epiclectic prayer. The people of Arcadia held onto the Epiclesis device with reverence: a sacred relic from Zeus’ ministry that allowed a mere human to not only speak to Zeus, but for Zeus to speak back. Lykaon arrived atop the mount and activated the device, eager to speak to the king of the kosmos.
From the device emerged an image which projected Zeus in all his divine glory, sitting atop a throne of gold! He wore brilliant and white robes of clouds that looked like a bright summer sky or a pure and beautiful sheep. And on his head sat a golden laurel crown. Truly he was as magnificent as a Graiac statue come to life.
Lykaon bowed his head and prepared to speak to his literal idol. Yet before he could, Zeus thundered out a demand.
“Whaddaya want.” Zeus grumpily demanded in a tone unbecoming of a god. It was actually more of a whining tone than anything; he had his arms crossed and his faced fixed in a pout as well.
Lykaon panicked! What had he done wrong? He’d gone through all the various expensive rituals the cult of Zeus had offered him. He’d bathed in the sacred waters; he’d proven his strength and heroism in their trials, and he’d donated much of his fortunes to them too. His sandals were off, right? What was left? The issue was that to Lykaon, this was the most important day of his life, but to Zeus this was just another annoying customer service call.
Most gods didn’t bother answering any of the endless requests from those whiny humans. Most paid proxy oracles or lesser gods to take calls instead. But Lykaon had gotten lucky: rather uncharacteristically Zeus had decided to take calls personally that day.
“I said, what do you want.” Zeus repeated himself more assertively. Lykaon looked up nervously and tried to plead his case.
“My lord, my people the Arcadians are soon going to war with our neighbours the Achaeans. We need your help in order to-” Lykaon began to say.
“Speak up!” Zeus thundered.
Lykaon practically jumped out of his skin as the giant projection yelled at him. Lykaon tried to repeat his plea, but the windy mountain air kept the device from picking much of it up.
“Where in my name are you? Are you on a mountain!? I can’t hear anything!” Zeus yelled back.
“Arcadia!” was all of Lykaon’s screaming that Zeus managed to hear.
But that was enough. In a magnificent flash of lighting, Zeus himself appeared in person atop his heavenly throne. He floated exactly where the hologram of him once did. At once the fierce mountain winds were stilled and the weather bent to the storm god’s will.
“Alright, I’m here. Now what the hell were you saying?” Zeus groaned as reclined in his seat.
Lykaon took a moment to collect himself. What an honor: an audience with Zeus himself in the flesh! To Lykaon this was the greatest day of his life, but to Zeus it was Tuesday. Seeing the god’s patience was wearing thin, Lykaon at once repeated the rehearsed plea he had.
“My lord, my people the Arcadians are soon going to war with our neighbours the Achaeans. We need your help in order to-” Lykaon began to say again.
“Arcadians? Oh great, I’ve been called out into the sticks. Couldn’t you have bugged Hermes or Pan instead? They’re better equipped for dealing with country bumpkins.” Zeus cut him off again. It had only just then registered to the snobbish king that he was out in the untamed rural frontiers that the rest of his noble class so looked down on.
“But my lord, you the Great Zeus are the greatest god! Surely it is only a test that you’d suggest we ask one of your inferiors over you oh great one.” Lykaon bowed deeper.
Zeus smiled. Brown nosing would get people anywhere with him. Pleased by this, Zeus stroked his beard and said, “Continue then, human.”
“Of course, sir. I am king Lykaon and my people the Arcadians are going to war with the Achaeans. In the upcoming battles I was hoping that you would bless our troops with your divine Power that we may overcome our enemies and avenge our past defeat. Just as you did in the days of the Titan war so that mortal men could wield divine strength, we ask that you let us follow in the footsteps of our ancestors.” Lykaon pleaded.
“Hm… tell me, for this epiclesis order did you purchase the gold tier subscription?” Zeus asked first.
“Of course, my lord.” Lykaon said.
“And you took the pilgrimage to bathe in the sacred waters in Olympia?” Zeus asked.
“Just last week milord.” Lykaon reassured him.
“And you proved yourself in the trials of heroism and strength?” Zeus asked.
“Most valiantly.” Lykaon grinned.
“…and you gave one half of all your wealth and worldly possessions to my cult?” Zeus asked.
“Without hesitation. I also knew to purchase the gold tier entry donation for each of the previous steps as well sir.” Lykaon assured him.
“Good boy.” Zeus nodded approvingly at his most recent mark. All those rituals were cheap to put on and profitable to keep running as long as zealot types like this moron kept taking them seriously.
“And I see you already took the sandals off, very nice. I like getting to say the whole ‘you tread on hallowed ground’ line, but I can respect a man who comes prepared. You clearly did your research before invoking me.” Zeus nodded approvingly.
“When consulting with the highest of all beings, no step can be overlooked. I am flattered by your compliments oh great one.” Lykaon knew exactly what to do.
“Flattery will get you everywhere little man.” Zeus chuckled to himself, thinking he was being clever.
“Then, in the upcoming war, we can count on your divine intervention?” Lykaon asked.
“Well, it depends. Are you going to be fighting just men or are you going to be killing women and children and babies and stuff too?” Zeus asked.
“What? Why does that matter?” a baffled Lykaon asked.
In those days butchering was pretty indiscriminate, so Zeus’ question sounded like an absurdity. Plenty of heroes butchered men, women and children with impunity. After all, Zeus was usually fine with giving the okay to slay utterly old and young while sparing no blood.
Nonetheless, after Lykaon dared question the god, Zeus’ expression soured, and the clouds became stormy. Lykaon prepared to beg for forgiveness, but as per usual Zeus spoke first. It wasn’t what Lykaon said directly, it was that he reminded Zeus what had him so grumpy in the first place.
“It’s not you. It’s public relations stuff, see. That bastard Hades is kicking up a stink about rules of war. So, Metis says it makes us look bad when we do war crimes or whatever. We have tone that stuff down, which means cutting down on killing civilians and following Hades’ stupid conventions.” Zeus grumbled.
“What? You’re the lord of the universe! Why do you need to follow any laws?” Lykaon cried in outrage.
“I know right! It’s those damned Hades people always sticking their nose where it doesn’t belong. Back in the good old days, I’d happily manipulate battles in your favor and then, oh boy if anything breathed it would die! Then any who dared defy Zeus the god of Graia would be reminded of their place!” Zeus thundered as the weather followed.
“Well, what is stopping you now, oh great one? Lend me aid and I could go on to conquer in your glorious name just as King Minos did.” Lykaon egged him on.
“Ah! Minos! Now that was a man after my own heart. All I had to do was point him in the direction of cities that defied me, and he’d exterminate them all! Classic, old school style heroism there!” Zeus laughed nostalgically for the bloody good old days, “I mean, have you ever met anyone from the city of Zakros?”
“No, lord. I’ve never heard of such a place. Is it even in Graia?” Lykaon noted.
“Not anymore! Play your cards right and soon people will say the same thing about the Arcadians.” Zeus winked.
“Achaeans! We will be fighting Achaeans m’lord!” The king of the Arcadians did his best to remain composed as he corrected Zeus in utter terror. That was a potentially disastrous mistake if left uncorrected. Zeus didn’t care.
“Yeah neat. Either way, getting the literally legendary Minos treatment shows you have big aspirations kiddo. I like that.” Zeus chuckled. Then his countenance sank as he remembered the Hades problem they’d just been discussing.
“…but unfortunately, that kind of good old-fashioned conquering is exactly what Hades cited directly. We aren’t allowed to do that kind of thing anymore. Hell is filling up with all the killing.” Zeus sighed. Lykaon trembled, unwilling to lose after all he’d sacrificed.
“Oh lord, why let them stop you? A king must always put on an act for the public, but behind the scenes who is freer than the ruler? Who is freer than you? A wolf does not ask, he takes. You work in mysterious ways so surely you could work… behind the scenes to handle our prayer… off the record.” Lykaon pushed his luck. Zeus smiled; he liked how this mortal thought.
“Y’know what, you’ve put me in a good mood, and it sounds like a great way to blow off some steam.” Zeus smiled as the sky brightened to match, “Let’s go kill us some Arcadians!”
“Achaeans! We will be fighting Achaeans; I am the king of Arcadia, who you will be helping.” Lykaon corrected him in a panic a second time.
“Yeah, whatever. Let’s get to it and- wait. Hold on. You nearly tricked me.” Zeus laughed. Lykaon was confused.
“I can help you out, but what’s in it for me?” Zeus leaned back in his throne.
“I thought that’s what all the rituals and donations were for!” Lykaon cried.
“That was to get me on the line. If you hadn’t done all that, I would’ve hung up a long time ago.” Zeus shrugged.
“I see… in that case, I assume you want a sacrifice of some sort?” Lykaon asked.
“What’ve you got?” Zeus leaned in eagerly.
“To you oh great one, I offer a sacrifice befitting your names as Zeus Meilikhios, the merciful, to you Zeus Polieus the protector, to you Zeus Xenios, the hospitable! I solemnly swear that in your name I will rule justly and piously. I shall share the light of your warmth with all my subjects and spread your holy name to all who pass through our city gates!” Lykaon declared.
A long, awkward silence passed between them. Lykaon awaited a response, but Zeus looked nonplussed. Lykaon prepared to continue before a violent and terrifying thunderclap knocked him back! An angered Zeus had clapped his hands together with a frown.
“This isn’t a sermon, it’s real life! Save that sappy shit for the temple priests, we’re talking business right now!” A very angry Zeus looked down on the little man who dared to think that would appease him.
“But great one… what is it you desire then?” Lykaon asked.
“Well… you yourself said that I am not restrained by any laws. Everything is so damned politically correct now; I feel nostalgic for the good old days. I’ve been on my best behavior for long enough; it’s time I get to cut loose. So, I expect an offering in that style.” Zeus declared.
“Lord… my vaults and wealth is empty from the donations.” Lykaon said.
“Boy, in the good old days, gods were not appeased with mere coin.” Zeus said as he leaned it.
“I’m… not sure I follow.” The desperate king lied in the hopes that he was misunderstanding.
“The point of a sacrifice is for a mortal to give up what they value most to a god. You’re the king of Arcadia, that means you’re the guy with fifty sons. People would notice if all of ‘em went missing, but… which son is your favorite?” Zeus began.
“Lord Zeus!” Lykaon’s instincts as a father jumped out momentarily but Lykaon the king restrained them.
“What… is the name of your favorite son?” Zeus asked again.
“My lord… you aren’t asking me to sacrifice my son, are you?” Lykaon pleaded as his heart sank.
“Hey! Hey! Hey! Whoa! Whoa! Whoa! I never said that. What I said was, you make me an offer. You give me a sacrifice and if it is something that pleases me, I will aid you. But if it is not what I want, I will not, and you go home empty handed. Now then, I just wanted to know the name of your favorite son.” Zeus explained.
“Nyctimus… he means the world to me.” Lykaon said.
“Nice name, Nyctimus. Anyways-” Zeus began.
“I get it lord! This is a test. How wise you are.” Lykaon laughed. There was no joy in that laugh, only desperation.
“Silence!” Zeus thundered at the audacity of this man to interrupt him, “Know your place, mortal! Even if I wasn’t a god, interrupting someone when they talk is just rude.”
“This is a test… right? You test my loyalty and then once I accept, you say the sacrifice is off, right?” Lykaon asked with the desperation of a man who was about to lose everything either way.
Zeus only glared down at him silently. Lykaon fell to his knees, but not to bow. He wept and wept as Zeus watched. The god’s bad day had just gotten a little bit better; after all this was why he’d chosen to take prayer calls today in the first place.
“A month, lord. Give me one last month with my son.” Lykaon said.
“Fine. But in a month, I expect my sacrifice. Then and only then will I deliver your people.” Zeus said.
Lykaon spoke no more. He wanted tear his tongue out for the last words he’d spoken. What had he just agreed too!?
“Well, great work team. That seems to take care of that and- oh. Remember, all this is off the books, Lykaon. You’re a smart man, you know how this works. Not a peep to anyone or else I really will be smiting Arcadians.” Zeus smiled.
With that he disappeared in a flash of lightning and returned to his abode on Olympus. Zeus immediately began to regret agreeing to wait. As soon as he returned, he was swamped with more condemnations from Hades and his faction pressuring the gods to curb their “inhumane cult practices” and “abuse of the mortals they were supposed to be protecting”, whatever that meant. What Zeus would have given for Typhon’s powers to travel through time.
Eventually the appointed day came and that was where Dike, the goddess of human justice found Zeus overlooking an assembly of Arcadians on their sacred mountain.
“Lord… is that a human sacrifice?” Dike asked as she noted the teenage boy tied to a pile of dried wood with the more usual offering of sacred cattle.
Zeus did not reply. He only watched. He’d just had another VERY bad day.
“Ah! I see, I must have stumbled on another one of your tests. Why do you keep doing these things, lord? You’d think a mortal being too willing to kill their child would actually reflect pretty badly on them rather than show their morality.” Dike mused. Zeus still did not reply.
“So, shall I go down and stop them or do you want to make the dramatic entrance yourself?” Dike asked. Zeus was as silent as the clear sky.
“Zeus?” Dike asked with a nervous laugh. She hadn’t called Zeus by name since the great Titan war.
“Shut up.” Zeus said without taking his eyes of Lykaon and the bound boy. The goddess of human justice felt a shiver down her spine. She had no authority to speak over divine justice.
So, the boy in his early teens was held by his father the king who held back tears. He went willingly: from a young age, he’d been brought up in the cult of Zeus and so this was a great honor. But what was this? Lykaon himself lit the fire. It was spreading. Why was Zeus letting it spread?
The boy was screaming and crying. First, he cried out instinctively for his god Zeus the lord of all that was just and good. Zeus still did not reply. Then the boy cried out for his father who stood right there, watching with the others. Dike dove down to try and stop it but Zeus slapped her away without taking his eyes off the spectacle.
Burning cows meant nothing to Zeus on paper. Zeus instead relished the starving giving up good food because they put Zeus first in their life. Burning people meant nothing to Zeus on paper. Zeus instead relished a father giving up his beloved child because he put Zeus first in their life. There was nothing humans could give Zeus that he did not already have. But when they suffered, the king knew that they acted not for themselves but only for him. That was what it meant to be king. That was what Zeus wanted as a god.
As the sacrifices died Dike wept just as the people did, but Zeus smiled, and the sky brightened. The Arcadians won their war with Zeus’ divine assistance. They defeated their neighbour and on some of the land they conquered, Lykaon founded a new city Lykosura where he established a cult of Zeus in honor of his patron deity. With all this Zeus was well pleased.
However, Lykaon’s wicked deed was soon exposed by Hades aligned anti-monarchists. When the allegations of human sacrifice were exposed, Zeus condemned him as an inhumane monster who defiled the sanctity of the gods with a cruel offering. Lykaon, was shunned and his reputation ruined.
To keep him from talking, Zeus turned him into a wolf, befitting his name. Supposedly this was done as punishment for offering his son as a sacrifice to Zeus. Then he killed any other witnesses to the offering who were suspected of leaking the truth to the public. It seemed that Zeus ended up slaughtering Arcadians after all. All was well and Zeus’ reputation remained untainted.
