*Myth 3: Wrath of Poseidon - The Old Man and the Sea

Long ago, humans hunted and enslaved mermaids. Before, many fishermen caught fish to survive, living in balance with nature. But one day they abandoned this compact with the sea when they learned enslaving and trafficking mermaids was more profitable. Rather than live in balance with the sea’s bounty they would subjugate it and enrich themselves. The old coot of the village warned them that they risked incurring the wrath of Poseidon: god of the sea and protector of merfolk. Greed blinded them and they ignored him.
Then the merfolk became scarce; they began to fear humans and the shores and so became harder to catch. Sailors had to sail farther out to try and find them. One boat sailed far from home and was on the verge of giving up. But then they found a solitary rock in the middle of the sea where an old man sat alone spear fishing. They asked the old man what he was doing here, and he replied in kind. They explained that they were here hunting mermaids; but were having no luck.
The old man with his strange spear explained the art of hunting at sea: he had come here for some good old-fashioned spearfishing. He told them knowing the movements of one’s prey and getting them to come to you was the art. If they really wanted to hunt mermaids, they should come back during the next full moon for a seasonal merfolk gathering. It would be the perfect time for an ambush. The larger the fleet of merfolk-hunters assembled, the bigger the haul would be!
The fisherman thanked the old man and spread the word gathering a multitude. The fishermen returned the next month, armed and ready to kill and capture to their heart’s content. However, the sea was very different from the first time they reached the rock. As they neared their final destination, a great storm and mist arose. However, they braved the storm to find the rock and the old man.
Though they could not see him in the bad weather they recognized his voice. The old man said to the sailors, “Very good. With so many of you here it shall be a bountiful haul.” They thanked him for the advice, inviting him aboard saying he deserved any mermaid he desired.
“Mermaids? I’m not here for mermaids.” The old man approached, walking atop the water and revealing his true form as he neared the horrified sailors.
Where once an unassuming old man sat now stood Poseidon god of the sea! His fishing spear was a glorious golden trident and his rags divine scale armor. His formerly calm eyes were now as furious as the thrashing blue waves. The sailors panicked, trying to flee the vengeful sea god with all their might.
The violent storm waves, cyclones and whirlpools grew worse as the sea god commanded! It was unlike anything the mortal men had seen before! The lucky ships that steered through the weather were quickly besieged by Poseidon’s Sea monsters! Sailing further out to sea only got one trapped between the legendary sea monsters Scylla and Charybdis! What were they doing here!?
“I kept my sea monsters far from the lands of men as a mercy. You have repaid me by invading my domain and so now the horrors of the sea shall come to shore.”
Horrific beasts from deep below rose up to feast on screaming sailors! Those who remained tried sailing back towards land. But on the shore stood the Cyclopes and Laestrygonians the massive, monstrous, man-eating sons of Poseidon.
“Do you think you can escape me on land?” Poseidon appeared on the shore, riding a monstrous steed, “The seas are my domain alone, but the world of men is held in common with Zeus from above and Hades below. It seems I must flex my influence more frequently in our common sphere to remind you.”
On Poseidon’s command, the Cyclopes and Laestrygonians hurled massive stones and sank many more ships; feeding their father’s fish. Then Poseidon himself joined in. Just as had been depicted on many mortals’ works of art, he hoisted an entire island in one hand, he threw it into the midst of the fleet, sinking all who remained in a great splash!
At the end of the slaughter only one man remained: the arrogant ship captain who had fallen for the god’s trickery and unwittingly invited many men to sleep with the fishes. All the storms and monsters loomed around him; the most terrifying of all: Poseidon stood on the water directly before him.
“I shall leave nothing to rumor. Return to the world of men: tell everyone what happened here today. There will be no second warning.” Poseidon motioned to a great wave like a loaded ballista. It was large enough to sink the entire city!
Poseidon branded the man with a stab of his trident: scarring him as an eternal warning to mankind. The tides washed the last sailor ashore where he did as was commanded of him. Ever after he never dared even approach the sea. But as he aged, he watched in horror as his warnings went unheeded. Once greed again tempted the next generation to ransack the sea. Left unchecked, would there be any survivors?
